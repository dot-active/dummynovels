Evidence, Part One
Fang Chuchu was dead. The police searched the apartment where she had lived, found evidence, and pieced together part of how she had carried out her crimes:
She had loaded the printer in the biology office in advance with white paper already printed with ten boxes, and then had Huang Lu print the list for her.
She had secretly changed her own name in Huang Lu's phone to the name of Huang Lu's husband, and in the middle of the night she had texted Huang Lu from her own phone, posing as the husband.
Before Huang Lu died, she had recorded Huang Lu singing that nursery rhyme on a phone and set the recording as the ringtone. The next day after school, she left that phone in the Class 4, Grade 4 classroom and turned on the light to lure Xiao Jin in. As the three women neared the classroom, Fang Chuchu, with her hand hidden in her pocket, used a second phone to call the one in the classroom — and so they heard Huang Lu's voice. Afterward, on the pretext of going to look for Xiao Jin in the restroom, she carried out the killing. Then she used Huang Lu's account to post the rhyme on the school forum.
The police also discovered that after Zhai Jia lost her mind, she had been sexually assaulted by Du Qiping. Later, at Fang Chuchu's instigation, Zhai Jia had poisoned Du Qiping to death……
The serial killings that had shocked society had finally come to an end. Fang Chuchu was dead, and Chen Daipeng lay in a hospital ward. He wasn't dead, but how he felt now was hardly better than death. The persona he had built had collapsed: the principal respected by thousands had suddenly become a suspect in rape and murder. He seemed to have aged more than ten years in an instant, and his usually sharp, shrewd eyes were now dull and unfocused.
The door of the ward suddenly opened, and an old woman came in, wearing gold-rimmed glasses, her face heavily powdered white. She sat down on the sofa and looked at Chen Daipeng in his bed with open contempt. "You beast. To do something like that."
"Who are you calling a beast?" Chen Daipeng, who had been ashen a moment before, suddenly flushed red. "Who the hell are you calling a beast?" He swept the glass off the bedside table onto the floor and roared at the woman on the sofa: "Calling me a beast? If I hadn't married a sexless old freak like you, would I have gotten so twisted I went after schoolgirls?"
The woman on the sofa didn't get angry. She only said coldly, "Now you complain I'm sexless. Back then it was you who begged me to marry you. Without my father's connections, could you ever have become a principal? You couldn't even have made night watchman. Apart from sex, I gave you all the power and status a man could want."
"That power and status mean nothing to me!" Chen Daipeng cut her off. "When I got that power and status, I finally realized that what I wanted most was a child — a child with my blood in its veins, who would call me Dad! The lowest, humblest person can have a child of his own. I had position and power, and I couldn't have one. Heh. And now my name is dirt, and I have nothing at all."
"Your name is dirt…" The old woman on the sofa looked contemptuous again. "You can lose face all you like, but I still have mine to think of." With that, she suddenly stood up and walked out.
Soon a man in a suit came in carrying a folder. He gave Chen Daipeng a very polite smile. "Hello, Mr. Chen. My name is Jin. I'm the attorney your wife has engaged to represent you."
Chen Daipeng didn't even raise his head, as though he hadn't seen anyone come in. The lawyer named Jin went on: "Mr. Chen, really, there's no need to be so pessimistic. I can help you."
Still without looking up, Chen Daipeng said listlessly, "Help me? Get my sentence reduced? A suspended death sentence, so I live a couple more years?"
The lawyer named Jin smiled. "It's not as serious as that. I've looked over the file. I can mount a defense of not guilty."
"Not guilty?" Chen Daipeng's eyes suddenly lit up.
"Yes, Mr. Chen. I'm not concerned with what you've done in the past. I've looked over the file, and I noticed that in this case, everyone who could have been a witness is dead."
