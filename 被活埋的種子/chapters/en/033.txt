The Hour Has Come
Hui Nan brought Zhai Jia back to the apartment she rented — a small, one-room place. The thought of sleeping in the same room as a mentally ill woman filled her with unease. But there were things Hui Nan badly wanted to understand. Why had Zhai Jia gone mad? What had happened the night before she lost her mind? To get anything out of someone who is mentally ill, you first have to build trust, and inviting Zhai Jia home was a quick way to do it.
First Hui Nan got out a set of clean clothes and had Zhai Jia take a shower. Though Zhai Jia had lost her mind, she could still look after herself well enough. When Zhai Jia had finished, Hui Nan showered too; showering as soon as she got home from work had always been her habit. She had planned to talk to Zhai Jia about that night once she was done. But when she came back into the bedroom, Zhai Jia was already lying on the bed, asleep. Hui Nan didn't wake her; no one knows what a sleeping madwoman might do if she's disturbed. Hui Nan had to give up, and lay down beside her, resigned.
The moment she lay down next to Zhai Jia, a terrible thought flashed through her mind. Officer Fei Qiang had said the real killer was one of the people who had attended the dinner after Huang Lu's death. One by one, the people at that dinner had died — yet Zhai Jia was still alive. Was she really mad, or only pretending?
In the middle of the night, Hui Nan was woken by the sound of the toilet flushing. In the faint light from the window, she saw that Zhai Jia, who should have been lying beside her, was gone. Gone to the toilet? Hui Nan lay under the covers with her eyes open and waited more than ten minutes, but Zhai Jia didn't come back. Outside the bedroom it was completely quiet; she couldn't hear a sound. Hui Nan threw something over her shoulders, got up, and went out of the bedroom. She wanted to know what Zhai Jia was doing.
The bathroom door was closed, and the light inside was off.
"Zhai Jia," Hui Nan called softly.
No answer.
Hui Nan had reached the living room. As she was about to switch on the light, she realized someone was standing in front of the light switch. The living room was very dark; Hui Nan couldn't make out the person's face, only a human-shaped black silhouette, both arms stretched straight out toward her, standing motionless in the dark.
The figure's strange posture frightened Hui Nan. Those outstretched arms looked ready at any moment to close around someone's throat. Hui Nan didn't dare go nearer. Like the silhouette, she stood frozen where she was.
"Zh-Zhai Jia?"
Trembling, Hui Nan called out again. The silhouette still didn't move.
Click! A light came on.
Not the living-room light — the bathroom light. Through the crack under the door, Hui Nan saw light suddenly spill out of the bathroom. Someone was in there!
Hui Nan felt as though her head would burst. Why was there a third person in her apartment?
The bathroom door slowly opened, and through the gap appeared a face as white as paper, with a mouth as red as blood.
It was Zhai Jia, her face painted with grotesque makeup.
"The hour has come. Let us be on our way," said Zhai Jia coldly as she stepped out of the bathroom.
By the light from the bathroom, Hui Nan glanced at the clock on the hallway wall. Both hands pointed to twelve.
The tension was so great that the very air around her seemed to have solidified. Hui Nan stared fixedly at Zhai Jia, ready to fight for her life at any moment. Zhai Jia, though, was perfectly calm. She walked slowly forward on tiptoe, passed right by Hui Nan, and stopped in front of the figure with the outstretched arms in the living room.
Click. Zhai Jia switched on the living-room light, and at last Hui Nan could see the figure clearly. Its face was even whiter than Zhai Jia's made-up face — so white it didn't look like a human face at all. Because it wasn't human. It was a plastic mannequin from a clothing store.
Hui Nan couldn't understand why there was a mannequin in her living room, let alone one in such a sinister pose.
"He's come to collect a life," Zhai Jia said suddenly. As she spoke, she had already lifted the mannequin and was heading for the front door.
The door opened, and a gust of cold, clammy wind blew in. Zhai Jia dragged the mannequin outside.
"Are you really mad, or just pretending?" Hui Nan didn't know herself why the question suddenly came out.
Zhai Jia, on her way out, stopped dead. She turned her head, gave Hui Nan an eerie smile, and said softly, "Actually — I'm the one who killed the fifth pockmark."
Hui Nan suddenly noticed that on the mannequin's pale face were five bright red spots.
