Another Coffin
In the small hours, Tiecheng No. 4 Middle School lay shrouded in darkness and dead silence. Only the windows of Class 4, Grade 4 glowed, out of place, with a deathly white light. Black shadows moved inside.
Zhai Jia was standing at the front of Class 4, Grade 4, facing the empty classroom. In front of the first row of desks lay a coffin. In the coffin lay a plastic mannequin with five red spots on its face.
Zhai Jia stared fixedly at the mannequin in the coffin, murmuring: "First pockmark hangs herself, second pockmark watches. Third pockmark buys medicine, fourth pockmark brews it. Fifth pockmark nails the coffin, sixth pockmark carries it…"
Suddenly footsteps sounded in the corridor outside. Zhai Jia glanced at the classroom door, her eyes full of boundless venom. Then she stepped down from the platform and lifted the coffin.
It was another overcast morning. As Hui Nan walked to school, several girls came running out of the school gate, shouting in panic: "Someone else is dead! Another coffin!"
The scene felt horribly familiar to Hui Nan — exactly like the morning, a little over a month earlier, when Jia Shi had been killed. In the middle of the sports ground sat a coffin, exactly like the one Jia Shi had been found in.
The body lying in this coffin wasn't a mannequin. It was Uncle Du from the gatehouse. Uncle Du was dead.
Wu Xian and his team soon arrived at the school again. According to the police investigation: the deceased was Du Qiping; occupation, gatekeeper at Tiecheng No. 4 Middle School; time of death, between 1:00 and 1:30 a.m.; presumed cause of death, poisoning.
The police also established that the coffin holding the body was the very one Jia Shi had asked Uncle Du to make. Uncle Du had died in the coffin he had built with his own hands.
At noon, Wu Xian withdrew his team once again. Unlike the previous times, when he had left empty-handed, this time he took a suspect with him: Zhai Jia. Many students testified that they had seen Zhai Jia carry this coffin onto the campus the day before.
Plenty of people thought being taken away would be good for Zhai Jia. In a detention center, at least someone would feed her and give her a bed, which was better than wandering the streets. But the police station gave Zhai Jia no refuge. After questioning her for an hour, they simply let her go.
Released, Zhai Jia naturally drifted back to the No. 4 campus — where, unluckily, she ran into Uncle Du's sister, Du Bamei. The school had notified Du Bamei, and she had hurried over to collect her brother's belongings. When this wailing countrywoman saw Zhai Jia, she suddenly shut her mouth and stared at her for a good half minute.
"Is she the madwoman?"
No one said anything.
Du Bamei had already guessed the answer. She suddenly rushed forward, seized Zhai Jia by the hair, and wept and cursed at once: "You rotten lunatic, today you'll pay for my brother's life!"
Zhai Jia, frightened and bewildered, let the countrywoman beat her. A crowd had gathered to watch, but no one dared step in.
When the two were finally pulled apart, there was a trail of blood on the ground. Du Bamei was clutching her arm in pain. At some point Zhai Jia had drawn a dagger from her waistband.
"Drop the knife!" Chief Wu's voice rang out from the crowd. Several police officers pushed into the crowd and moved the watching students and teachers back. Du Bamei, too, was helped away.
Zhai Jia stood alone, gripping the dagger, gazing blankly at the three armed officers surrounding her, apparently unaware of any danger.
"Throw the knife away!" Hui Nan's voice rang out from the crowd being moved back.
Zhai Jia looked at Hui Nan, smiled a little, and began walking toward her, chanting as she went:
"White Stone Mountain, flecks of snow.
Snowflakes drift on the morgue below.
White, white beds; red, red sky…"
Bang! Bang! Two shots. Two bullets struck Zhai Jia's body. The dagger fell to the ground, and Zhai Jia's body fell onto the campus.
"Zhai Jia!" Hui Nan ran over and lifted her up. Blood was already soaking through her chest. She had been shot through the heart.
Her lips trembling, Zhai Jia went on chanting the rhyme:
"White, white beds; red, red sky.
A pointed spade will send you on high."
Zhai Jia was dead. She died on the sports ground. She died with her eyes open. As she died, chanting that eerie rhyme, her eyes were fixed on one person in the crowd: Zhang Yao, the politics teacher of Class 4, Grade 4.
