Buried Alive
When the wolfhound appeared near the summit, Zhu Hua had chosen to run in the opposite direction from Hui Nan. The dog chased Hui Nan down the mountain, and Zhu Hua ran up to the top.
There was no view to speak of at the summit of White Stone Mountain — only a jumble of dead trees blocking the line of sight and two towering boulders. Between the boulders ran a long, narrow path leading to the graveyard on the back slope.
The sky was overcast, which made the narrow path between the rocks seem even darker and more sinister. The cramped space was thick with damp and rot, and every so often a chill wind blew through. Zhu Hua almost wondered whether the path led to hell. What lay at its other end was not hell, but to Zhu Hua it was every bit as terrifying. As she stepped out of the narrow path, the sight before her brought one word flashing into her mind:
"Morgue."
On the flat ground of the back slope stood ten iron beds, neatly arranged in two rows of five. Much of their white paint had flaked away, exposing dark red rust. Every bed was covered with a white sheet, and each sheet bulged with the outline of a human form. It wasn't hard to guess there were people lying under them. Looking more closely, Zhu Hua saw that of the ten beds, eight had a figure under the sheet; the other two were empty.
Zhu Hua went up to the first bed. A card was stuck to the rail at its foot, with two characters on it: "Jia Shi."
Zhu Hua took a deep breath, reached out, gripped the sheet, and yanked it back. She saw a pale face, bright red lips, and wide, staring eyes. Under the sheet was a plastic mannequin, with five red spots on its face. The fifth pockmark?
Zhu Hua moved to the second bed. The card at its foot said "Zhai Jia." She pulled back the sheet: another plastic mannequin, this one with six red spots on its face — and this mannequin was female.
Zhu Hua went to the third bed. Zhang Yao: a male mannequin, seven red spots on its face. The fourth and fifth beds were empty; the cards at their feet said Zhu Hua and Hui Nan.
One after another, Zhu Hua pulled back the sheets on the remaining beds. Under each lay a mannequin, and the cards at their feet said Ma Dahua, Huang Lu, Xiao Jin, Wang Qin.
Only the last bed was left. The card at its foot bore two characters: Suo Xin. Had Suo Xin, who had fled to another city, been killed too? Step by step, Zhu Hua walked toward the last bed. Like the others: a white sheet, the outline of a human form. Zhu Hua seized the sheet and threw it back.
Zhu Hua saw the face under the sheet. It was not a mannequin's face. It was Wang Qin's acid-burned face. As the sheet came away, Wang Qin's mouth slowly stretched wide. She was smiling. It was the most horrifying smile Zhu Hua had ever seen. Normally when people smile they open their mouths and narrow their eyes; but this face of Wang Qin's, even as its mouth stretched wide, kept its eyes staring round and open.
Zhu Hua felt her legs go weak. Then Wang Qin suddenly sat bolt upright on the bed, her grinning mouth almost at Zhu Hua's throat.
"Ah!" Zhu Hua screamed and stumbled backward without thinking. She had gone only a few steps when she trod on a sheet of plastic — with nothing under it. Her body plunged down into a pit more than two meters deep. At once the plastic sheet under her feet dragged the surrounding earth with it, pouring into the pit like running water.
The pit wasn't wide, only deep. In moments half of Zhu Hua's body was buried. She struggled to climb, trying to break free of the earth. Of course she couldn't — because it was Wang Qin who had dug the pit, and Wang Qin who had set out the beds, and all of it was so that Zhu Hua would fall into that pit. By now Wang Qin had got down from the bed. From under the sheet she pulled out a shovel, came to the edge of the pit, and began shoveling earth into it, one spadeful after another. Zhu Hua was not dead yet. She was about to be buried alive.
