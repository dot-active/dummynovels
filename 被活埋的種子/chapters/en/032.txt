The Madwoman
It was Monday morning again. In the mathematics office, a young woman sat in front of Hui Nan, talking without pause.
"Think about the stuff this school teaches. How much of it is actually useful? How many students will ever use what they learn here?"
Hui Nan: "……"
"How many research professors or mathematicians will this whole school ever produce? Most of them will end up as cooks, barbers, ordinary jobs like that, right? The lucky ones become accountants or cashiers. So why does so-called basic education insist that every single student know how potassium permanganate produces oxygen, how many eyes a spider has, how to recite the equation of a parabola?"
As she talked, she picked up an algebra textbook from the next desk and flipped it open at random. "Look at this. It teaches you how to work out a square root by hand. I honestly can't think what use that is to an ordinary person. How many times in a lifetime do you need a square root? And when you do, what are the odds there's no damn calculator anywhere around? Huh? What! Are! The! Odds!" Midway through she flew into a rage and slammed the textbook down on the desk.
"You learn all this stuff basically for the exam, and once the exam's over you forget the lot. Take geography class," she said, breathing hard for a moment; then her tone quickly went calm again.
"They force us to memorize this country's ethnic groups and that country's religions. If you want to learn things like that, you have to take the students all over the place and let them experience the local cultures for themselves. That's the only way it sticks; that's what learning is. And look at our geography teacher. Her name's Zhai Jia — 'Zhai Jia,' like zhai jia, stuck at home. What geography are you going to learn stuck at home!"
"Rrring — rrring —"
The young woman was in full flow when the bell for class suddenly rang. She stood up reluctantly. "Let's talk later, I have to go to class. It's history this period, and that young teacher Jia Shi is kind of cute, so it won't be too boring. Hee hee hee hee hee."
With that, she skipped out of the mathematics office. Hui Nan stared after her, too shocked to speak. Because that young woman was Zhai Jia.
Zhai Jia had gone mad. No one knew why. At first everyone felt sorry for her, but before long the sympathy turned into fear. Every day the madwoman drifted around the campus like a ghost, and at any moment she might appear beside someone and say something that made their skin crawl.
Once, two teachers went into the restroom and found Zhai Jia inside, talking to the mirror above the sink. "Teacher Xiao, you don't look very well today…"
The two teachers fled in terror. They didn't dare check whether Xiao Jin was really in that mirror, because they knew it was in front of that very mirror that Xiao Jin had died.
Another time, Zhu Hua was in the corridor when Zhai Jia suddenly called to her from behind. "Teacher Zhu Hua, Teacher Wang Qin asked me to pass on a message. She says that of the four homeroom teachers with exam classes this year, three are already gone. You're the fourth…"
Zhu Hua had already gone white. She clutched her lesson plans to her chest and fled without looking back.
Many of the teachers began contacting psychiatric hospitals, but none of them would take Zhai Jia. Psychiatric hospitals only took two kinds of people: those whose families paid the fees, and those who, like Fang Chuchu's mother Tang Sui, made trouble at government offices.
Zhai Jia was neither. She was truly mad and would never go and make trouble at a government office, and her only family in Tiecheng was her ex-boyfriend, who had long since abandoned her.
And so this young woman was abandoned by all of society. She lived among human beings but had been cut off from human civilization, and day by day she grew more wretched — clothes in disarray, hair loose and tangled, face caked in grime. Her one piece of luck was that she had a decent colleague: Hui Nan.
One afternoon, as Hui Nan was getting ready to go home, she saw Uncle Du from the gatehouse using a broom to drive Zhai Jia back from the entrance of the teaching building. After being blocked twice by the broom, Zhai Jia suddenly burst into tears and screamed, "You heartless bastard! When you wanted me in your bed you acted like a gentleman, and now you turn on me and throw me out!" Hui Nan supposed she had mistaken Uncle Du for her ex-boyfriend. Uncle Du turned scarlet at the abuse and brought the broom down hard on Zhai Jia. Hui Nan couldn't stand to watch any longer. She went over and caught hold of Uncle Du. "Enough, enough, stop hitting her. I'll take her out."
Hui Nan took Zhai Jia by the hand and led her toward the gate, but Zhai Jia kept struggling to get into the building. Even a madwoman knows hot from cold, and knows to spend the night somewhere with heating.
"Come with me. You can sleep at my place tonight."
At those words Zhai Jia suddenly stopped struggling and stood there, stunned. "Sleep at your place?" A faint glimmer of joy appeared in her vacant eyes.
Hui Nan would very soon regret this rash, impulsive invitation. Spending a night with a mentally ill person is in fact a truly terrifying thing.
