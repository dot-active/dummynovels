Static
Tiecheng — "Iron City" — got its name from the mountains beside it, rich in iron ore. A steelworks had been built near the mines; it dug the ore and smelted iron, brought the town prosperity, and brought it pollution too.
White Stone Mountain lay near the steelworks. It should have been a green and lovely mountain with clear streams, but the thick rust that drifted over from the steelworks had corroded it into a barren mountain no one ever visited.
Zhu Hua and Hui Nan had already entered that barren mountain. The sky above them was veiled by a layer of red rust, and the air smelled of rust and mold. Underfoot lay pitted white rubble — the rubble that gave White Stone Mountain its name. Sadly, the snow-white stones of the past had long since had their cracks filled with black grime, and the black-and-white ground looked like the static on a television screen — a scatter of snowy flecks — that left you irritable and uneasy.
"Red, red sky." "Flecks of snow." The scene before them couldn't help reminding them of the rhyme Zhai Jia had chanted as she died. They were now certain that Zhai Jia had been here before she died. The rhyme seemed to be a guide leading them toward the truth. But the closer they came to the truth, the closer they came to horror.
They kept climbing. The way grew harder and harder underfoot; the ground was littered with tangled dead branches and dry grass higher than their knees. They didn't know how much longer the path would go on like this. All they knew was that their destination was the graveyard on the back slope, where many of the dead were buried.
They climbed the trackless mountain for a long time. Just as they neared the summit, a beastly roar suddenly came from above, and then a dark shape appeared in the dry grass, racing down the slope toward them. The shape in the grass drew closer and closer, and when it was about five meters away it suddenly leaped out of the grass and lunged at Hui Nan. It was a big black wolfhound.
"Ah!" Hui Nan screamed and turned and ran in terror. A bite from a dog like that would leave you dead or crippled. Heedless of the branches tearing at her legs, she ran madly down the mountain for her life. The wolfhound barked ferociously and stayed right on her heels.
Humans are no match for dogs in agility. Halfway down the mountain, Hui Nan finally tripped over a branch, rolled over several times, and slammed into a large tree. Dazed by the impact, she couldn't get up. In an instant the wolfhound was upon her, leaping for her throat.
Crack! The sound of breaking bone.
When Hui Nan came to her senses, she saw Fei Qiang from the police standing in front of her. The wolfhound that had been chasing her lay dead at his feet.
Hui Nan pressed a hand to her pounding chest, gasping. "Thank you for saving me. How did you come to be here?"
Fei Qiang: "Since Zhang Yao died, I've been protecting you in secret."
Hui Nan gasped for breath a while longer, then took out her phone to try to reach Zhu Hua, but there was no signal at all on the mountain.
Hui Nan: "Can you contact whoever you sent to protect Zhu Hua? Find out whether she's safe right now?"
Fei Qiang stood there and didn't move.
Hui Nan was astonished. "Don't you need to check on your colleague and on Zhu Hua?"
Fei Qiang sighed. "No one is protecting Zhu Hua. And the police didn't send me either."
Hui Nan: "Then you…"
Fei Qiang: "I'm not a police officer anymore. I resigned yesterday. Protecting you is my own decision."
Hui Nan: "Wh-why?" Hui Nan dimly sensed that things inside the police were even more complicated than at the school.
A look of pain crossed Fei Qiang's face. "Because I found out the truth. The truth about how Jia Shi and Zhai Jia died."
