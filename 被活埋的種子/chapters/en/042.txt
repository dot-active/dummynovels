Is It Over?
White Stone Mountain, a barren mountain no one ever visited, had claimed two lives in a single day. Zhu Hua, the English teacher, had been buried alive; Wang Qin, the chemistry teacher, had fallen from a cliff. And it still wasn't over. There was one more person at the summit at that moment: Chen Daipeng, who had just hurried over from the school.
Chen Daipeng had driven to White Stone Mountain and wandered the slopes for a long time without finding anything. At last he climbed to the summit and heard Wang Qin's final scream. Following the sound, he made his way around to the back slope. There he saw an open-air morgue, and a girl in a school uniform standing at the edge of the cliff with her back to him. Chen Daipeng didn't dare show himself; he hid behind a rock and watched in secret.
The girl in the uniform spoke to the cliff: "Why did you have to kill her? She never killed anyone. She only wanted to find out what happened back then." At the sound of the girl's voice, Chen Daipeng felt a chill go right through his body. When she had finished, the girl turned around. A gust of icy wind blew up, and through her windblown hair Chen Daipeng saw her face.
Chen Daipeng's pupils contracted sharply. At the same time, he was certain of two things: this girl was Fang Chuchu, and this Fang Chuchu was not human.
Having turned around, "Fang Chuchu" began to walk toward the rock where Chen Daipeng was hiding. Chen Daipeng felt a fear he had never known. Crouching behind the rock, he clutched the talisman paper and wooden sword he had brought in his pocket just in case. Halfway there, "Fang Chuchu" suddenly stopped, crouched down, and began digging in the earth with her hands.
What was she digging? Her own grave? Was she going to bury herself in the ground, go back to where she belonged — and then wait for her chance to crawl out of the earth and harm people again?
This all had to end! Chen Daipeng suddenly found his courage. He couldn't let this "Fang Chuchu" go back into the earth; today he had to settle this. He pulled the talisman paper and wooden sword from his pocket and burst out from behind the rock. "Die, demon!" He hurled a fistful of talismans into "Fang Chuchu"'s face. As she flung up her hands in panic to fend them off, a wooden sword plunged into her neck — the other end of it gripped in Chen Daipeng's hand.
Clutching her neck, "Fang Chuchu" looked at Chen Daipeng in agony. Chen Daipeng's whole body jolted. Meeting those eyes was like looking at himself in a mirror. Terrified, he let go of the wooden sword and stumbled back two steps.
"Fang Chuchu" stood up and kept staring straight at him. Chen Daipeng couldn't bear to meet her gaze. He turned, took the scented oil from his backpack, and poured it over "Fang Chuchu." Then he took a lighter from his pocket, hesitated a moment — and in the end threw the lit lighter at the oil-soaked "Fang Chuchu."
Chen Daipeng had done his homework before coming: he knew that ghosts fear fire, and that a corpse can only rest in peace in the earth once it has been cremated.
"Fang Chuchu" was now ablaze from head to foot, lying on the ground and howling in agony.
That pitiful howling gave Chen Daipeng the illusion once again that the sound was coming from him — that it was he who lay burning on the ground. He couldn't bear to look, couldn't bear to listen. Covering his ears, he fled down the mountain as fast as he could. He ran all the way to the bottom without stopping, and just as he stepped through the gate of White Stone Mountain, the howling finally stopped. But in the instant it stopped, Chen Daipeng felt an overwhelming sense of loss, as though he had lost something very precious.
Perhaps there are no ghosts in this world; perhaps all ghosts come from within the human heart. Either way, it seemed to be over. Chen Daipeng got safely away from White Stone Mountain and went home. He tried hard to forget the day — the eyes that had stared into his, the agonized howling. He needed a good rest: the next day the school was holding its pre-exam rally for the fourth-year students. But he couldn't sleep. He lay awake all night.
The next day he dragged his exhausted body to school, his throat so sore he didn't want to speak. At the pre-exam rally, he had Hui Nan speak in his place. Zhu Hua hadn't come to school, and Hui Nan had become the only surviving teacher of the fourth year.
On the platform, Hui Nan stood beside Chen Daipeng holding the microphone, facing the whole school, teachers and students. But she didn't say a single word. Instead she suddenly collapsed to the floor, covered her face with both hands, and began to sob.
The whole school was stunned by her strange behavior. Chen Daipeng, beside her, quickly reached out to help her up. "Teacher Hui, Teacher Hui, what are you crying for?"
The sobbing stopped abruptly. Hui Nan took her hands away from her face. On it was an eerie expression, half a smile and not a smile, and she said something that filled the whole school with dread:
"It wants to climb back out of the earth."
