The Bray of a Live Donkey
Late that night, in the gatehouse of the No. 4 teaching building, Zhang Yao's phone rang. No one answered it, because Zhang Yao was in the classroom of Class 4, Grade 4. He sat on a chair in the front row, his hands tied behind his back and a rope looped around his neck. The other end of the rope was tied to the back of the chair, so that he had no choice but to keep his head tilted back.
Zhang Yao hadn't been killed after he was knocked out. He was in the Class 4 classroom, strapped to a student's chair, head up, hands behind his back, like a well-disciplined pupil.
On the platform facing him stood a figure. The classroom was very dark, and Zhang Yao couldn't tell who it was until it began to speak: "Dean Zhang, you're awake?"
Gu Qing's voice! It was like a basin of cold water poured over his head; he shook with cold. "Y-you… you… you're…"
"Dean Zhang, do you know what 'the bray of a live donkey' is?" Gu Qing's voice was light and lilting, but what she said made the flesh crawl. "It's the name of a dish. You cut the meat off a living donkey and roast it on the spot while it's still alive." As she spoke, Gu Qing picked something up from the lectern and walked toward Zhang Yao.
Zhang Yao fixed his eyes on her hand. As the object gradually emerged from the darkness, he nearly fainted with terror. In Gu Qing's hand was the spade Principal Chen Daipeng used for digging and planting flowers — a sharp, pointed spade.
"White, white beds; red, red sky.
A pointed spade will send you on high."
Zhang Yao suddenly remembered the rhyme Zhai Jia had chanted as she died, and felt himself a step closer to death.
Emerging from the dark along with the spade was Gu Qing's face — but it was not a human face. Gu Qing's entire head was made of paper.
Gu Qing came up to Zhang Yao and said mournfully, "Like Teacher Ma, my neck was strangled until it broke. That's why neither of us has a head."
"But Dean Zhang won't end up like us." Gu Qing's voice quickly returned to calm. "And you have a chance to live — if you can answer a question correctly."
Gu Qing's words rekindled Zhang Yao's will to live. People will always choose to believe whatever works in their favor. So Zhang Yao forced himself to stay as calm as he could, hoping to seize this thread of hope and leave this place alive.
"Wh-what question?"
"Dean Zhang, I want to know: why, every day at morning exercises, did you make us stand in perfectly straight lines? You wouldn't allow the slightest crookedness."
For a moment Zhang Yao was dumbfounded that at a time like this he should be asked such a strange question. He had been discipline dean for more than ten years and lectured students almost every day; answering a question like this was naturally easy. First he steadied his terror, then said as loudly as he could:
"Straight lines are what show the school's spirit and morale. You can't see it from down there, but standing up on the platform you can see it all clearly. When the ranks are neat it looks very pleasing, and if they're not neat — ah! No! Aaah!"
Amid Zhang Yao's string of screams, the spade in Gu Qing's hand had already gouged a chunk of flesh out of his belly. Gu Qing waited until his screams had sunk to moans, then said coldly, "More than a thousand of us stood down there every day, through bitter cold and blazing heat, just so the three or five of you on the platform could enjoy a pleasing view? You'd better answer again. Give me an answer I'm satisfied with."
Terror and pain had brought Zhang Yao to the verge of collapse, but to survive he had to think fast about a question he had never once considered. Everything he knew, from ancient times to the present, from China and abroad, flashed through his mind, and at last he seemed to find something he could use. He gave his second answer.
"Standing in neat ranks evolved from ancient times. When soldiers went into battle, their formation had to be orderly to be effective. If the ranks were crooked, not only could they not mount an effective attack, they could easily wound their own side."
"Aah — not again! Aaah —" Zhang Yao, who no longer had the strength to scream, let out another heart-rending shriek: the pointed spade had gouged into his belly a second time.
Gu Qing's voice grew colder still. "We don't need to learn how to form ranks for battle."
The searing pain finally robbed Zhang Yao of the ability to think. Tears and snot streamed down his face, and with what strength he had left he begged over and over: "I can't answer! Please, let me go!"
"Then I'll give you an answer." Gu Qing held the pointed spade. "The truth is, whether our lines were straight or crooked meant nothing at all. You forced us into neat lines every day with that vicious attitude only to teach us obedience — obedience to everything. You made us do pointless things just to show off your own authority."
On any ordinary day, Zhang Yao would have hit the roof at words like these. But today he didn't dare argue — just as, when he berated students, they didn't dare argue back. Not only did he not argue, he kept nodding. "Yes, yes, yes. It's exactly as you say. Schools love all this formality. But I'm only an employee; I didn't make these rules. Anyone else as discipline dean would do the same. You can't pin this on me!"
When Zhang Yao had finished defending himself, Gu Qing spoke again. "Then I'll give you one more chance. One more question."
The classroom fell silent. Zhang Yao hoped to seize this last chance. The silence lasted about a minute. Then the paper Gu Qing said slowly, "That night, you and I……"
