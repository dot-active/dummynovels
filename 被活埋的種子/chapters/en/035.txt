Conversation Outside the Gatehouse
The serial killings were still going on, and Zhai Jia's death had become the most terrifying link in the chain. With the other deaths, people could guess a murderer was at work; but Zhai Jia's death had been pure accident. In the first case, what they faced was a killer. In the second, it meant they were facing Death itself.
Fear gripped the No. 4 campus once more. Everyone worried that Death's finger would point at them next. The most frightened of all was Zhang Yao, because Death had given a hint: the eyes of the dying Zhai Jia.
Worse still for Zhang Yao, the principal had decided he should spend that night at the school. The school needed someone to stand in temporarily for Uncle Du at the gate. There were a lot of computers in the building, so someone had to keep watch overnight, and Zhang Yao — one of the few male teachers at the school, and the discipline dean besides — was the only option.
The killing spree had already begun. This night was never going to pass peacefully.
When school let out, teachers and students alike fled as if for their lives, and Zhang Yao was left alone on the empty campus.
After hours the campus was deathly still. The teaching building was pitch dark; only the desk lamp in the gatehouse gave off a faint light. Zhang Yao sat on the bed across from the lamp, playing with his phone to pass the long night. He didn't dare sleep. But as the hours crept by and the night grew deeper, drowsiness pressed harder and harder. Just as he was drifting between sleep and waking, he faintly heard two men talking. Their voices came from far away and drew steadily closer:
"Young Jia, what do you think of this coffin I made?"
"Uncle Du, you did a really fine job. It's very comfortable lying in it."
"So long as you're comfortable lying in it."
"And you, Uncle Du — are you comfortable lying in yours? How does it feel to die in a coffin you built yourself?"
"However comfortable a coffin is, it's not as comfortable as a bed. Your Uncle Du would rather die in bed. I built that bed of mine myself, too. Lying on it, I feel safe."
"That bed of yours — looks like someone's on it right now."
"He ought to leave. If he doesn't, he'll die on that bed."
Zhang Yao started awake, wide awake at once. He went over the conversation he had just heard, looked down at the bed beneath him, and in terror rolled off it onto the floor. The conversation had sounded far too real to be a dream. It was as if the two speakers were standing right outside the gatehouse window.
Zhang Yao flung open the gatehouse door and ran into the lobby of the teaching building, where there was a door leading out to the sports ground. He rushed to it, ready to unlock it and escape — and then stopped dead. In the pale moonlight outside, he could see clearly through the glass: two figures were standing right in front of the door.
Every hair on Zhang Yao's body stood on end. He turned and ran, scrambling up the stairs, and dashed for the discipline office on the second floor. At the office door his hands shook so badly he couldn't hold the keys; he clutched the whole bunch in his fist and couldn't find the right one, so he had to throw his body against the door. Luckily it wasn't locked, and it burst open under his weight. Once inside, he quickly locked the door and pressed his back hard against it, gasping for breath. He had never been through anything so terrifying.
Still panting, Zhang Yao forced himself to calm down. His first thought was to call the police. But when he reached into his pocket for his phone, his heart went cold. The pocket was empty. His phone was in the gatehouse, on Uncle Du's bed.
Fortunately, there was a landline in the discipline office. Zhang Yao slowly eased himself away from the door and crossed to the desk by the window where the phone stood. At the window he leaned out and peered toward the entrance of the teaching building. The two figures were gone.
Where had they gone? Had they vanished? Or were they already inside the building?
Zhang Yao didn't dare think any further. He snatched up the phone on the desk. But when he put the receiver to his ear, his heart went cold again. Where there should have been a dial tone, there was nothing at all.
Someone had pulled out the phone line! Who? And where was that person now? Suddenly Zhang Yao remembered something: he had been the last to leave this office today, and he clearly remembered locking the door behind him. Yet just now, when he crashed in, the door hadn't been locked.
Zhang Yao suddenly realized how terrible this was: someone had been in here — and that person might still be in this very room. In a panic he groped for the desk lamp.
Click! The light came on. A figure was standing right in front of him. It held a picture frame in both hands, and the frame hid its face. Zhang Yao knew that frame. There were many like it in the discipline office, holding portraits of Marx, Stalin, Mao Zedong and the like. But the frame before him now held the memorial portrait of the dead language teacher, Ma Dahua.
Fear shot through his body like an electric current. A memory from a little over a month earlier flashed through his mind: the campus had been hung with Gu Qing's memorial portraits, and he had ordered Ma Dahua to change the pictures back — and that same night Ma Dahua had been killed. Had all the ghosts who had died on this campus come back to life?
Slowly, the figure holding the portrait moved it aside, and at last Zhang Yao saw the "person" behind it. This "person" was wearing the clothes Ma Dahua had worn in life — and above the collar, there was no head.
The "person" who had come into the discipline office was a corpse come back to life.
"Aah!" Zhang Yao screamed and lurched backward without thinking, only to trip over a chair in the dark. The back of his head struck the corner of the desk hard, and he fell to the floor. In the instant before he lost consciousness, he saw "Ma Dahua" walking toward him.
The campus at midnight is a space reserved for the souls of the dead.
