Chapter Fourteen: Regent of the Realm

After the autumn of 1646, when the Longwu regime fell, and the end of that year, when the Shaowu regime fell, in the first month of 1648 Zhu Changqing was installed by Zheng Chenggong as Regent of the Realm (Jianguo), and the era name was changed to Dongwu.

The days of relative peace that Zhu Changqing and Wu Jingyu had in Fuzhou lasted only about a year. When the Longwu regime fell and he fled to Guangzhou, a mere month later the Shaowu regime fell too, and he fled Guangzhou again. In the drifting and wandering, Zheng Hongkui took Zhu Changqing into his army as military supervisor, while Wu Jingyu's family temporarily settled in civilian houses outside the camp.

In the end, the thing Wu Jingyu had feared most came to pass.

Seeing the Hongguang, Longwu and Shaowu regimes fall one after another in a short time, even princes who merely would not surrender might meet disaster at any moment, to say nothing of one who became regent, or founded a regime.

One chilly spring evening, Zhu Changqing came in plain clothes to Wu Jingyu's residence. He had been reckoning in his mind that, since his position as regent was unlike before, he could at last legitimately give Wu Jingyu the rank of consort, and so had come to seek her consent. But once she became an imperial consort, their lives would be bound still more tightly together, and in his heart he was in fact deeply torn.

Seeing Zhu Changqing, Wu Jingyu was naturally glad, but what worried her more was his taking the title of regent. She knew Zhu Changqing was not a man who craved imperial power; it was only that the times pushed people forward and shoved them to the crest of the storm, and the Prince of Huai, Zhu Changqing, could not but shoulder the responsibility of restoring the Ming's realm, especially as he had always been a man who did not shirk responsibility.

The Ming needed him, so he bravely took on the duty, unafraid of life or death.

But the Hongguang Emperor had been executed after capture, the Longwu Emperor killed after capture, and the Shaowu Emperor had hanged himself after Guangzhou fell. These cautionary examples made it rare for Jingyu to sleep soundly, and every day her heart was seized by panic.

"This commoner woman Jingyu pays obeisance to Your Majesty," said Wu Jingyu, kneeling to kowtow.

Seeing Wu Jingyu, who had never knelt and kowtowed to him, now on the ground with her head bowed, Zhu Changqing hurriedly lifted her up, with a pained look. "Whether Changqing is Heir, prince or emperor, Jingyu will always be Jingyu, and need not perform such grand ceremony."

"But Your Majesty..." Wu Jingyu wanted to say more, but Zhu Changqing drew her into his arms and said softly, "I am still used to you calling me Your Highness. You know that, unless absolutely necessary, I have no fixation on the throne. I have always wanted to give you a rank, and now that I have taken the title of regent, no longer bound by a prince's marriage regulations, I can fulfill this wish, so I came to ask what you would like."

Zhu Changqing continued, "But I also know that once you are made a consort, you and I will share one fate in life and death, and I cannot bear that..."

Wu Jingyu stayed quietly in Zhu Changqing's arms for a while before saying softly, "If Jingyu were alone, I would surely be at your side and share life and death with you; but I have two sons, who are surnamed Wu and not Zhu. That was my parents' insistence from the start, and it was for that very reason that my father declined the Prince of Huai's palace's proposal. To this day, I cannot disregard the Wu family..."

Hearing this, Zhu Changqing's heart was full of mixed feelings. He held Wu Jingyu tightly for a long time before slowly saying, "How hard it is to untangle the affairs of this world! If not for the spirited impulse of youth that made me break regulations by leaving Raozhou city to visit Jingdezhen incognito, how would I have met you? And if not for the fall of the country and the loss of home, where would I have had the chance to return to Jingdezhen and be reunited with you? Having at last, by taking the title of regent, escaped the prince's restrictions on taking a consort, your responsibility to the Wu family remains, and I face threats to my life at any moment. Fate always contends with people, and people are powerless to resist. As in our youth, each of us still bears a heavy burden of our own, and our destiny is truly ill-starred."

Zhu Changqing knew in his heart that he had set out on a road of no return. Jingyu was no ordinary delicate woman who lived by depending on a man. She had opinions, judgment, brisk capability, and had managed the Wu household for more than half her life; she was a woman of foresight.

This was the Jingyu in his heart forever.

If speaking of love, each could walk through fire and water for the other without hesitation; and in this age of fallen country and wandering exile, what was needed most was cool and calm thought. He understood her considerations: if one day the Ming realm could be restored, with no worries behind her, then formally enthroning Jingyu would be the best choice for her.

Though the chance that such a day would come was very slim...
