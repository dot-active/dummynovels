Chapter Nine: Parting Feelings

On the morning of the eleventh day after Zhu Changqing and his attendant arrived in Jingdezhen, Li Zong packed up the luggage and took it along, preparing to board a boat back to the palace later. The porcelain shop was not far from the wharf, so the two went to the shop to wait there until the boat sailed, and also to say farewell to Wu Jingyu and everyone in the workshop.

Hardly had they sat down when the commotion outside grew louder. Looking closely, they found that the townspeople, having learned that the Heir was about to leave Jingdezhen that day, had come out to join the excitement. In these days the townspeople and the Heir had grown familiar, and ostensibly they were seeing him off, but in truth every extra glance was precious, and everyone was reluctant to part.

More and more people arrived until the front of the shop was packed so tight not even water could seep through, and in the end Boss Jiang of the pastry shop "started it," walking straight into the shop and saying, "Wrap up four blue-and-white tea bowls and four small cake plates for me." As he spoke, he waved in greeting to Zhu Changqing.

"Heir, come again when you have time. This commoner knows the Heir loves osmanthus cake best, and will be waiting for the Heir's next visit at any time."

When the crowd outside the shop saw that buying a few things inside meant they could speak with the Heir at close range, they pushed in one after another; those with more money bought several pieces, and those without bought a small spoon or a chopstick rest or the like. In short, the crush spread from outside the shop to inside, frightening Zhu Changqing out of his seat again, and he could only step forward to help and chat with the townspeople.

Zhu Changqing wrapped the porcelain unskillfully, his hands and feet rather clumsy. The older women saw him flustered and didn't hurry him; this way they could talk with the Heir a little more, and kept praising how handsome he was, so that Zhu Changqing could only smile shyly, not forgetting to tell everyone to watch their step, which brought still louder cheers.

"Look, the Heir and Miss Wu are both wearing pale blue cloaks today. Standing together they look just like the golden boy and jade girl attending Guanyin. How beautiful!"

Hearing this, Zhu Changqing and Wu Jingyu instinctively looked at each other, and it was better not to have looked, for they both burst out laughing. Today they had indeed been of one mind.

Wu Jingyu's hands were busy beyond measure, yet her mouth would not miss its last chance to "make fun of" Zhu Changqing. "The Heir is practically the god of wealth of the Wu porcelain shop. If I had known, I would have dragged you to show your face in the shop more often and bring in the money."

"That can help make up for the hospitality of your household all this time," Zhu Changqing said, and this time he was no longer bashful but had returned to his usual steady manner.

After wave upon wave of people had poured in and everyone had bought about what they wanted, the time for the boat to sail was near. Zhu Changqing bowed to the townspeople present, thanking everyone for their care over the many days, and then took out a folding fan and handed it to Wu Jingyu. Wu Jingyu opened the fan, and inside was Zhu Changqing's own calligraphy: "The phoenix cries out, on that high ridge; the parasol tree grows, on that sunlit slope," signed with his courtesy name, "Xiaxin."

Yes! He was that Heir of Huai, of broad bearing, who knew what he would do and would not do as an imperial prince.

"It can't repay the cost of meals, but keep it as a memento."

Jingyu nodded. The fan was a light gift but weighty with feeling, and it made her seem to see again the Heir who had talked so grandly of Yu Qian beneath the parasol tree when they first met. She turned and told Zhi'er to fetch the osmanthus cake she had specially gone that morning to buy from Jiang's pastry shop, and gave it to Zhu Changqing.

"So the Heir likes osmanthus cake too? It happens to be Jingyu's favorite as well. It was still warm when I bought it, just right for the Heir to eat on the road, and to let the Princess Consort try. Jiang's pastry shop has been run by three generations, with good, honest ingredients and a flavor beyond reproach. I wanted it for myself at first, but hearing the Heir likes it too, besides both wearing pale blue cloaks, we've now found one more thing we agree on. Please accept these osmanthus cakes, Heir."

"But a gentleman does not take what others love," Zhu Changqing said, a bit embarrassed, bringing out even "gentleman" to protest.

"They're only a few pieces of osmanthus cake, and you make them sound like rare treasures." Wu Jingyu laughed and went on, "Mere osmanthus cake can be bought again, it's no difficulty. Perhaps the palace cooks can also make superb osmanthus cake, but how could it match the lively kindness of the people of Jingdezhen?"

Unable to refuse Wu Jingyu, Zhu Changqing could only accept it solemnly and hand it to Li Zong. The sailing time was drawing near, and Zhu Changqing, leading Li Zong, bowed in farewell to everyone. Wu Jingyu and the crowd saw the two off to the boat together, so that a long human dragon stretched all the way from the porcelain shop to the wharf.

Everyone watched from the riverbank as Zhu Changqing and his attendant boarded. Wu Jingyu saw the roundel patterns woven into the back of the pale blue cloak, appearing and vanishing in the sunlight like flowing ripples, and a sense of loss she couldn't put into words welled up in her heart. Though Jingdezhen was not far from the Prince of Huai's palace, the two worlds almost never touched. After today's parting, who could know whether they would ever meet again?

Her eyes grew misty.

When the boat had gone some distance from the wharf, Zhu Changqing, as if remembering something, turned and looked toward Wu Jingyu on the faraway riverbank and waved to her, signaling her to go back to the porcelain shop. Wu Jingyu curtsied to Zhu Changqing and waved back, softly saying, "Goodbye," before stepping away to walk back. Watching her grow smaller in the distance, Zhu Changqing found, without noticing, that tears had welled in his own eyes too...
