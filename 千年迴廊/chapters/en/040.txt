Chapter Three: Welcoming the Guest

The great commotion in the porcelain shop was finally settled, thanks to Zhu Changqing's "mediation." Wu Jingyu, knowing that Zhu Changqing was the Heir of the Prince of Huai, invited him to stay in the shop for tea, out of courtesy. Zhang Huan, who had no wish to be held back any longer, took his leave of the Heir and slipped away in a flash.

As the crowd dispersed, only the usual bustle of coming and going remained on the street. Zhu Changqing studied the shop's furnishings closely. On both sides, huanghuali wood shelves stood in staggered heights, blue-and-white vases, jars, cups and plates set out in order, the bluish-white glaze setting off the warm tones of the wood, stretching all the way to the back of the shop; beneath the window stood a table and chairs, tea smoke curling up from the low table, which added a touch of warmth to a room full of porcelain colors.

Zhu Changqing gathered up his robe and sat down with an elegant bearing, lifted a tea bowl and took a few light sips, then raised his eyes to Wu Jingyu and asked, "How is the porcelain business?"

Wu Jingyu had been watching the refined and yet noble Zhu Changqing before her with some fascination, and it was a while before she came out of her daze to reply, "Thank you for your concern, Your Highness. The Wu family has run the blue-and-white trade for three generations, and is already the foremost porcelain merchant in Jingdezhen."

"So I can see." Zhu Changqing nodded, set down the tea bowl, and smiled at Wu Jingyu. "Father Prince likes to drink Songluo. Miss Wu entertains me with this fine Songluo tea, served in blue-and-white porcelain bowls besides. I feel quite at home, as if I were in the princely palace."

This little joke was meant to ease Wu Jingyu and draw the two closer. And Wu Jingyu did smile; she felt the Heir put on no airs and was very approachable. From the moment he had stood up for justice until now, he had been mild and gentle, like a spring breeze.

"You're called 'Jingyu'? Which 'jing' and which 'yu'?" Zhu Changqing gestured with a hand for Wu Jingyu to sit.

"In reply to Your Highness: the 'jing' is the one in 'scenery,' and the 'yu' is the one in 'a land that nurtures talent,'" Wu Jingyu answered as she sat down.

"A good name... You have studied?" On hearing Wu Jingyu use the phrase "a land that nurtures talent," Zhu Changqing sensed intuitively that this girl could do more than merely read.

"In reply to Your Highness: Jingyu has learned to reckon accounts and keep books, and my father also engaged a tutor for me, so I have studied for several years."

Zhu Changqing nodded again and continued, "The Zhang family is a great tea merchant of Fuliang. Knowing Father Prince loves Songluo, they send tea leaves to the palace every so often, and Father Prince is used to buying from them, so Zhang Huan and I know each other. I never expected to meet him here today and be recognized at a glance. I came incognito to see how the people live, not wanting to alarm the local officials or interfere with their lives, and he went and staged this scene. It's turned out quite contrary to my wishes."

Wu Jingyu laughed again. Of all things, big merchant houses like theirs had the best-informed ears. With people coming and going every day, from south and north, a few casual words of chat would tell of every strange tale and anecdote from every place; even if they didn't learn of it at once, in the end it could not escape their ears. Some years ago, the affair of the Prince of Huai doting on a concubine and neglecting his wife, even meaning to replace his heir, had grown so serious that local officials had to bring charges before it stopped; they had all heard something of it, though there were many versions of the details and outsiders could hardly know the truth. Now, seeing the Heir, upright, pure and noble, with the people's welfare in his heart, she truly could not understand how the Prince of Huai could be so befuddled. Look at the two of them, both legitimate eldest sons: what kind of character was Zhang Huan's, compared with this? The difference was as great as heaven and earth.

"Does Jingyu know how the people of Jingdezhen actually live, behind its seeming prosperity?" Zhu Changqing still cared most about how the common people truly lived, and this was also a main purpose of his coming to Jingdezhen.

"In reply to Your Highness: there is much work here. The work in the kilns is mostly hard labor, but life there is at least warm and fed. Besides that, there are many other places that hire hands, so many people come from elsewhere. Some enter the kilns, some work for merchants, and some load and carry cargo or work the boats. In short, there's a livelihood to be had." Wu Jingyu gave a brief outline of Jingdezhen's livelihoods, and Zhu Changqing listened attentively.

For a moment, an inexplicable feeling of respect and fondness rose in Wu Jingyu's heart. She had followed her father in business since she was small, and was no fool at judging people. Zhu Changqing's eyes were full of sincerity, not feigned. This much she could see.

Just as they were talking, a sound of gongs and drums came from far off and drew near, and before long the doorway of the porcelain shop was packed with people again. But this time the arrivals were of a rather special standing: it was none other than the Prefectural Assistant of Raozhou, stationed at Jingdezhen and also overseeing the firing of imperial porcelain. He dismounted, a large sedan chair and several bearers following behind him, and hurried flustered into the porcelain shop. On seeing Zhu Changqing he dropped to his knees with a thud and kowtowed. "Liu Biaoren, Prefectural Assistant of Raozhou, pays respects to Your Highness the Heir. How could Your Highness leave the city and come to Jingdezhen privately, without sending word for a memorial? If the Censor hears of this, Your Highness will surely be called to account by the court, and this humble official's black gauze hat will likely not be saved either. I beg the Heir to move to the official residence, and let this humble official entertain you properly and think carefully about how to respond."

Zhu Changqing frowned. As expected, once his identity was exposed, trouble followed. Liu Biaoren was still kneeling with his head bowed and didn't see the frown; he only worried that this little ancestor of an Heir was making a private visit against regulations, and with one head swollen to two he was truly flustered and distraught.

Zhu Changqing first told him to rise, then said sincerely, "I am only traveling incognito and never meant to alarm anyone. What offense have you all committed? I'll simply stay at an inn, and you may consider that I never came."

At this, Liu Biaoren grew still more anxious.

"The Heir is of precious station. How can he lower himself to stay at an inn? Wouldn't that cut this humble official's life short?"

"I'm only staying at an inn, where I can also observe the people's lives. It can't shorten Assistant Liu's life," Zhu Changqing insisted.

"No... that won't do." Liu Biaoren was still worried about his own career. A private visit was already a breach of regulation, and staying at an inn besides, he, Liu Biaoren, no matter how bold, would never dare go along with it.

Zhu Changqing's face had become somewhat troubled. The two were at a stalemate like this, and Li Zong, standing in attendance, didn't know what to do either. Just then Wu Jingyu seemed to grasp something, and spoke up. "Since both the Heir and the Honorable Official have their concerns, the Wu family has a separate courtyard, with a rather quiet setting, regularly tidied by our servants, so it's clean and comfortable too. It may not match the grandeur of a princely palace or an official residence, but if the Heir doesn't mind, he is welcome to move to the Wu family's garden residence."

Liu Biaoren was quick-witted too. Hearing this, he looked as if he had seen a savior, and said eagerly, "Excellent! I will report that the Heir has come this time to inspect how well the Prince of Huai's blue-and-white ritual vessels are being prepared, and so staying at the Wu residence is most fitting. Only, would it be too much of an imposition on the Wu household?"

"Not at all. To host the Heir will make the Wu residence shine!" Wu Jingyu rose smiling and bowed to Zhu Changqing and Liu Biaoren. Though only sixteen or seventeen, she took a hint at once. It turned out the Heir was not permitted to leave Raozhou prefectural city without authorization, and it seemed the blame was no small matter, one that would implicate even him, the Prefectural Assistant of Raozhou. So Liu Biaoren's smoothing things over for the Heir was also a way of getting himself out of trouble, and with a good surface reason in hand, he could simply give his account. And she herself had become the best choice for untying the knot.
