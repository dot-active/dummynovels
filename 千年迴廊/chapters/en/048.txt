Chapter Eleven: Reunion

Not long after Boss Jiang walked into the Wu porcelain shop, a woman in a lotus-pink cloak with hidden woven gold patterns and an indigo moon-glow skirt came out through the shop door and walked toward Zhu Changqing and his party, light flowing at every step, followed by a maid in blue-green of about the same age. Both looked to be around forty.

As the arrivals drew nearer, Zhu Changqing saw clearly that they were Wu Jingyu and Zhi'er. Wu Jingyu's cheeks seemed fuller than twenty-odd years before; though she was now forty, she still kept seven or eight tenths of her former radiance. Only, after the grinding and settling of the years, the air of command of the head of a household had grown stronger still.

The dozen or so sworn retainers were wary at first, but then heard Li Zong bow and say, "Greetings, Madam Wu," and one after another they bowed and greeted her too.

"Jingyu pays respects to Your Highness, Guard Li, and all you brave men." Wu Jingyu, leading Zhi'er, curtsied in return.

"Twenty-odd years without meeting, and how is it that Your Highness has become so..." Jingyu could not bear to go on, and only looked at the weariness of Zhu Changqing, covered in smoke and dust, with a look of aching pity. Li Zong saw tears in Zhu Changqing's eyes, and that he only gazed fixedly at Wu Jingyu, as if the moment he opened his mouth the tears would spill over.

"I'm sorry... to come see you only now, in this state," Zhu Changqing finally spoke.

"It doesn't matter... Jingyu can still give you a place to shelter from wind and rain, just as twenty-odd years ago. The Wu garden residence has been empty all along, as if it knew that one day Your Highness would come." As she spoke, Wu Jingyu's own eyes grew hot and wet.

"Come with me, quickly." Wu Jingyu turned and led the way; she feared that if she did not turn, the indignity of her tear-wet face would meet Zhu Changqing's eyes.

Twenty-odd years. Two people who should have had no meeting had come together again because of the Qing troops' entry through the pass. She had known back then that the Princess Consort had sent a matchmaker to ask for her hand, and so she knew the Heir had her in his heart; her father, because she could only take a husband into the family, had declined the position of Heir's consort, leaving only regret for the two of them. When the fifteen-year term in the marriage contract ended, the live-in son-in-law had left the Wu household, taking a large sum of Wu money, and gone elsewhere to set up a family and career. Her eldest son was now eighteen; since her father's passing she had effectively managed the household for over a decade, yet through an unexpected turn of fate she was meeting the present Prince of Huai in this way.

Seeing Zhu Changqing's disheveled, grimy state, it seemed the identity of a prince was the heaviest burden this age of the Ming's fall had laid upon him. In peaceful times he had given his all for the people, doing many things that were not for a mere idle prince to do; when the Ming's great edifice toppled, the princes of the clan instead became the first to bear the brunt.

Walking into the Wu garden residence, the parasol tree in the main courtyard seemed to have grown even more lush. Zhu Changqing halted and stood for a long while, looking at the tree, and sighed deeply. "Even trees are like this; how can a man bear it?"

He thought of the princely palace already burned, and the parasol tree there must long since have turned to ash. At once an indescribable bitterness filled his heart.

The youth who had once talked so radiantly beneath the parasol tree about Yu Qian had now walked Yu Qian's road himself: never to surrender.

Because he would not surrender, amid the ravages of war, his uprising had failed, his family had been scattered and driven to wander, going their separate ways, and from then on he had become a target the Qing army was eager to sweep away.

"Jingyu still remembers the Heir back then, speaking of Yu Qian with such radiant spirit. Now that Heir who held the Ming in his heart and worried for the country and the people is still here, in the hearts of the people and in Jingyu's heart."

Jingyu looked at Zhu Changqing, lost in thought before the parasol tree, her heart moved: admiration, and even more, bitterness and an unwillingness to let go. Why should such a good Prince of Huai end like this?

Zhu Changqing gave a wry smile at this. As a prince of the Zhu clan, he should hold to integrity even more than Yu Qian. But since Emperor Chengzu's time, princes had held no political or military power. In the turn of dynasties, how could an unarmed prince face the iron cavalry of the Qing and the great talents of the Regent Dorgon?

"It is not Your Highness's fault. You have done more than enough for the Ming and the people of the Ming."

Zhu Changqing turned and looked at Wu Jingyu, and gave her a faint smile. "Thank you for consoling me, a man whose country is broken and family lost."

"It isn't consolation, it is assessment." Wu Jingyu gave Zhu Changqing a faint smile in return, and went on, "What Your Highness needs now is to wash and rest, not consolation. Zhi'er, hurry and tell the servants to heat water for Your Highness, Guard Li, and all the brave men to bathe, and prepare the beds. Just now Jingyu already told my son to go and fetch a cook, so when Your Highness has rested, there will be hot food and rice."

"Jingyu is as attentive as twenty-odd years ago," Zhu Changqing couldn't help praising.

"Shouldn't I be more attentive? If I were just the same as twenty-odd years ago, wouldn't Jingyu have gone backward?" Jingyu gave another faint smile and teased Zhu Changqing.

"Not only more attentive; even your ability to tease me has grown with the days, more and more polished." Zhu Changqing laughed heartily, and his long-furrowed brow finally relaxed, so that Li Zong and the dozen or so sworn retainers, seeing it, also eased their tightly drawn nerves a little.

While they were talking, servants brought in hot water, tea leaves, a pot and bowls, and Jingyu said again, "Your Highness has had a hard journey. First drink a few mouthfuls of Songluo tea to moisten your throat." Then she stepped into the room, directing the servants in setting up and brewing the tea.

He never could have imagined that at the most desperate moment of his life, he would still get a mouthful of Songluo tea. Zhu Changqing watched Wu Jingyu directing with such assurance, his heart full of mixed feelings. Fortunately he had not married Jingyu back then; at least she had not been burdened by him. The country was broken, but her home remained, and at the most critical moment she had given him the greatest warmth like timely rain.

Who could foresee the changes of the human world?

Notes:
1. Moon-glow skirt (yuehua qun): a women's skirt popular in the late Ming and early Qing, made by joining many panels of different colors; as the wearer walked, the hem fluttered and the colors flowed like moonlight, hence the name.
2. In the Ming dynasty, taking a son-in-law into the bride's family took two forms: the "elder-support son-in-law" and the "fixed-term son-in-law." The former lived long-term with the wife's family and supported her parents; the latter agreed in the marriage contract to a term of residence, after which he could leave the wife's family and set up a separate household.
