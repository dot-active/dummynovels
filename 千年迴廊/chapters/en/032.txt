Chapter Seven: Ulterior Motives

Just a few days later, a "new customer" appeared at Kangnawu's spice shop — none other than Bai Jue, whom they'd met at the Hu tavern. Spotting Su He inside the shop from a distance, he brightened at once, his face lighting up with a broad smile.

"Miss Su." Bai Jue stepped into the spice shop, first paying his respects to Kangnawu and his wife, then letting his gaze settle on Su He as he greeted her cheerfully.

"Come to buy spices, Twentieth Young Master?" Su He looked at him, puzzled. Bai Jue had never once patronized Kangnawu's spice shop before — Kangnawu and Miwei had only met him for the first time at the tavern that other day — so how was it that only a few days into their acquaintance, he'd come all this way specifically to buy spices?

"Indeed. I heard the proprietor mention that day how well-stocked the shop was, and having some free time today, I thought I'd come take a look." Bai Jue answered Su He, and his eye caught sight of the brush in her hand, a sheet of paper spread on the desk, the ink in the inkstone already beginning to dry — a clear sign she'd been mulling something over for quite a while without setting brush to paper.

"What is it you're trying to write, Miss Su?" Bai Jue seized the opportunity to offer his eager assistance.

"A letter home." Su He looked ruefully at the paper on the desk. "I've been away from home so long now, I really ought to write and let them know I'm safe. But there's so much I want to say, and on top of that, I only know so many characters — I can manage a ledger or an order slip well enough, but I don't even know where to begin with a proper letter."

"I know a little of letters myself. If you'd permit it, Miss Su, I'd be glad to write it for you." Bai Jue bowed again as he offered.

"How could I possibly ask that of you? Wouldn't it hold up your spice shopping?" Su He waved the brush in her hand, sounding unconcerned.

"The spices aren't urgent at all — I was only wandering about at my leisure anyway. Besides, writing a letter home won't take very long. For me, it's really no more than a matter of picking up the brush and dashing it off."

"Well, if you say so." Su He handed him the brush, musing aloud, "A scholar heading to the capital for the examinations must surely write beautifully. Since you're offering to take up the pen for me, I'm only too glad for the rest."

Bai Jue took the brush, sat down at the writing desk, added a little water to the inkstone, and ground the ink fresh before finally asking, "What would you like to say, Miss Su?"

At this, Kangnawu and his wife exchanged a glance. They could both see plainly enough what little scheme Twentieth Young Master Bai was up to — from the tavern to the spice shop, there was no doubt he'd come purely for Su He's sake, and buying spices was likely nothing more than a pretext. So they didn't bother interfering, content for now to watch how he'd manage things on his own.

Su He thought for a moment, then began dictating on her own: "Father, Mother: The shop belonging to A-Cheng's family has taken excellent care of me, and I've become close with Uncle Nawu and Aunt Miwei here at the West Market of Chang'an. Your daughter is doing well in Chang'an — please don't worry."

Bai Jue listened with a smile. "Miss Su speaks so genuinely, so plainly, without any dressing up at all — allow me to polish it just slightly." No sooner had he said this than he added, "Who is A-Cheng?"

"We grew up together since childhood," Su He replied without a moment's thought, then pressed him at once, "Just write it down as I said, Twentieth Young Master — why do you ask?"

Rebuffed gently, Bai Jue didn't dare press further. Feigning full concentration, he dipped his brush and, in one smooth stroke, wrote: "To my beloved father and mother: Since arriving in Chang'an, your daughter has been treated with the utmost care by everyone at A-Cheng's family shop, and has had the good fortune to become acquainted with Uncle Nawu and Aunt Miwei, with whom we visit often and get along splendidly. I now want for nothing in food or clothing, and all is well — Father and Mother need not worry themselves on my account."

As Bai Jue's brush flew across the page, Kangnawu and his wife exchanged another glance. They had originally felt a little concerned for Su He, but it seemed she was no pushover at all — so the two of them simply settled back to enjoy the show.

Su He took the finished letter from Bai Jue and read it aloud word by word, her heart brimming with delight. "So this is what it means to be a man of letters," she couldn't help but exclaim.

"Not at all, not at all — Miss Su is too generous with her praise." Bai Jue kept up his show of modesty, only to see Su He suddenly change the subject entirely.

"To show my gratitude, what spice would you like to buy today, Twentieth Young Master? Let me recommend one for you. That borneol camphor there suits a gentleman best — it clears the mind and sharpens the senses when you smell it, wonderful for studying."

Bai Jue gazed at Su He's radiant, smiling face and found himself momentarily at a loss for words. Buying spices had only ever been a pretext — he'd meant to pick up something small and be done with it, never expecting Su He to take it so seriously that she'd bring out the shop's "prize treasure" and set it right before him. And she'd phrased it so graciously besides, that outright refusing seemed impossible, while buying it cheerfully would sting his purse rather badly — the whole situation was thoroughly awkward.

While he hesitated, Su He added, "I'll have them give you a discount, sir — this is a special treat, just for you."

"Well then... a little, perhaps." Bai Jue forced out a faint smile, feeling his coin purse deflate all at once...
