Chapter Nine: The End of Bai Jue

Du Cheng had originally planned to take Su He back to Jinguan City on this trip, but Miwei mentioned that her daughter and son-in-law were expected to arrive in Chang'an in a little over a month's time. Since Su He and Kangnanaizi were close in age, and Miwei had come to think of Su He as a daughter of her own, she wanted Su He to stay and get to know the two of them, and to personally introduce Anmoruo to Shu brocade herself, so as to lay the groundwork for a smooth, lasting partnership going forward. Miwei had originally invited Du Cheng to stay as well, but he had to hurry back to Jinguan City so as not to delay the next batch of Shu brocade orders bound for Chang'an. In the end, it was decided that he would come back for Su He another time, and he set out with no small measure of uneasiness weighing on his heart.

At the mere mention of Kangnanaizi, Miwei's whole face lit up with warmth. She hadn't seen her daughter in the better part of a year and missed her dearly. Su He, too, was deeply curious — what could a Sogdian young lady, close to her own age yet so capable, so accustomed to traveling far and wide with the caravans, possibly be like? Su He herself longed with all her heart to step outside Jinguan City and see the world beyond, while Kangnanaizi had already seen skies far wider than her own — and the thought filled her with equal parts envy and admiration.

* * *

But before Kangnanaizi and her husband could arrive, the daughter of the Jingzhao Prefect stormed into the spice shop, trailed by her maidservant, seething with fury.

"What shameless little vixen dares to seduce the son-in-law the Jingzhao Prefect himself has chosen?" Her expression was ugly, and her words uglier still.

"I'm not sure who our honored guest might be, nor who this son-in-law of the Jingzhao Prefect is meant to be. This young lady's words come out so garbled and foul, one might mistake her for some common shrew." Su He had never met the Jingzhao Prefect's daughter before, and being subjected to this tirade of abuse left her baffled as well as more than a little annoyed.

"What blind, ignorant creature dares speak to her like that? This is none other than the eldest young lady of the Jingzhao Prefect's own household, and Twentieth Young Master Bai is the son-in-law the Prefect himself has chosen." The maidservant, without waiting for her mistress to speak, jumped in first, using her mistress's authority to "introduce" the two of them and their standing.

"Twentieth Young Master Bai is merely a customer who comes here to buy spices. Perhaps the young lady is under some misunderstanding?" Miwei stepped forward to smooth things over and ease the tension.

"Misunderstanding?" the young lady scoffed coldly. "Carrying on at that Hu tavern is bad enough, but now chasing after spices at her doorstep too? Don't tell me nothing's going on between them — I wouldn't believe it for a moment."

"How absurd. His legs are his own — where he chooses to buy his spices is none of anyone's business. We run an honest shop here, and any customer who walks through the door is welcome; there's no reason on earth to turn people away."

Having failed to gain the upper hand, the young lady's face flushed red with fury.

"My sources are excellent, so don't bother trading words with me. From today onward, you are to stay far, far away from him — or you'll regret it."

Su He laughed in sheer disbelief and shot back, "Tell him to stop coming around, then. This is our shop — what right do you have to tell me to stay away from him? We can't afford to entertain such an 'honored guest' as yourself."

"As if we'd ever want to set foot in this wretched place again." The young lady's face changed at once.

"What a relief that would be." Su He smiled, watching the furious young lady, and cupped her hands in a mocking bow. "Do keep him on a tighter leash, and don't let him loose to trouble people again."

"You—" The young lady, at a loss for words, stamped her foot hard on the ground and stormed off in a fit of rage, sleeves flying.

At the time, Kangnawu happened to be away from the shop, leaving only the three women there. The Jingzhao Prefect's daughter, baring her teeth and puffed up with arrogance, had come looking for a fight, and for a moment Miwei stood frozen, unable to get a word in. But watching Su He send the woman packing without so much as a flush in her cheeks or a hitch in her breath, Miwei finally let out a breath of relief and stepped forward. "I've heard the Jingzhao Prefect himself thinks highly of Twentieth Young Master Bai's literary talent — he's gone several times to present his writings for the Prefect's consideration, and was even received into the mansion itself. A-He, you really do have nerves of steel. That young lady of the Jingzhao Prefect's household is notoriously willful and overbearing — which merchant doesn't show her some deference? Yet when these two young ladies crossed paths, our own Miss Su stayed perfectly composed, and there was no contest at all."

Su He gave her own chest a mock pat and laughed. "The son-in-law the Jingzhao Prefect has chosen? That's still a long way off — it won't count for anything until he actually passes the jinshi examination. As for that willful, overbearing young lady, she gave me quite a fright, I'll have you know — my heart's still pounding away."

At this, the shopgirl finally worked up the nerve to chime in too. "Miss, you were magnificent — how could you possibly be afraid? If our young master hadn't already gone back to Jinguan City, seeing this, he'd probably wake up laughing in his dreams."

Su He glanced at her sidelong, patted her shoulder, and said approvingly, "You really are devoted to your master's household, aren't you? Seems his wages haven't been wasted on you after all."
