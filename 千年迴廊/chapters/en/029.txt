Chapter Four: Spotting the Fake

Du Cheng nodded, satisfied. He had no idea, of course, what was really going on in Su He's mind, and thought only that he had successfully coaxed her out of her huff, so he took her hand again and they walked on.

They passed a jewelry shop, its window a dazzling display of pendants, bangles, and earrings made from turquoise, agate, crystal, and pearl, each piece gleaming in its own color — enough to overwhelm the eye. Su He only gazed at it all from a distance, showing no sign of any intention to step inside.

"Not going in for a look?" Du Cheng asked.

"No." Su He shook her head. "What's the use of looking if I'm not going to buy? We ought to watch our spending — cut back on anything unnecessary. Money doesn't come easily." Then she took Du Cheng's hand and pulled him onward.

"All right, I'll do as you say. Once we're engaged, I'll be sure to buy you something." Du Cheng could see plainly enough that Su He loved jewelry and ornaments, yet held herself back for knowing how hard money was to earn, and it tugged at his heart a little to see it.

"Is that so? Then I really do have something to look forward to, when it comes to our engagement!" Su He broke into giggles, bright and clear, and Du Cheng found himself laughing along without quite meaning to.

Amid their laughter, from somewhere far off, Su He caught the faint drift of one fragrance after another — some light and elegant, others warm and mellow — floating just at the edge of her senses.

"That's spices." Su He's eyes lit up. She loved spices back in Jinguan City too, but she'd never imagined the spice shops of Chang'an's West Market could be so grand in scale!

Su He had always had a fondness for benzoin, its warm, rich fragrance the very thing that captivated her most, and without a second thought she walked straight into the spice shop. Her eye fell at once on two glass bottles set on a wooden shelf — one holding chunks of amber-brown benzoin, the other filled with pale, lustrous borneol camphor. She stared happily at the benzoin, while also studying the glass bottles themselves with curiosity — glass was a rare and precious thing indeed! Crystal clear, it let one see exactly what was held inside.

"What sort of spice do you like, A-Cheng?" Su He turned back to ask Du Cheng, who had followed her into the shop.

"No use for it on the road, hauling brocade — it'd be a shame to get it wet in the rain. But when I'm talking business, I do like to burn a bit of agarwood."

"Well, since we're here, shall we have a look together?" Su He said.

A Hu woman came over, welcoming them warmly. Not far off, a Hu merchant stood talking with another traveling merchant.

"What can I show you, my lord, my lady? What catches your fancy?" the woman asked.

"What's this?" Su He asked, pointing at the borneol camphor.

"That's borneol camphor," the woman replied, and went on, "A truly rare spice, this — the royal family and the nobility are especially fond of it, and it's hard to come by in an ordinary spice shop. Even here we keep only a small amount in stock, reserved for our regular customers."

Hearing this, Su He felt she had learned something new. Perhaps the spice shops back in Jinguan City carried borneol camphor too, but being so scarce and precious, it was likely kept carefully out of sight, not easily shown to just anyone; here, though, it sat displayed in a glass bottle, visible at a glance, its color and form plain to see.

Seeing how keenly interested Su He was, the woman opened the bottle's stopper. "Would you like a smell?"

At that, Su He fanned the scent toward her face with delight, and a cool, refreshing fragrance rushed into her nose, leaving her feeling instantly clear-headed and refreshed.

Just as she was about to say something more, the traveling merchant nearby unrolled a bolt of cloth, showing its pattern to the Hu merchant.

"Take a look at this Shu brocade — the sheen, the vivid patterns," the merchant said, quite pleased with himself.

At the words "Shu brocade," Su He's gaze drifted instinctively toward the unrolled bolt of cloth. She looked at it for a moment, then turned back toward Du Cheng.

"That's not right." Su He shook her head. "Real Shu brocade has a finer sheen than that."

Du Cheng, ever steady after years of hard work out in the world, privately agreed that Su He was almost certainly correct, but he didn't dare say so outright. To his surprise, the merchant's face changed at once, and he snapped angrily, "What would some rough country girl know about Shu brocade, running her mouth off like that in here?"

"Who's a rough country girl? You're the one who can't recognize quality when it's right in front of you — I've woven Shu brocade for years, and you're the one running your mouth off, if you ask me." Su He, displeased, shot right back without a shred of hesitation.

"What did you say?"

The merchant's face showed a flicker of unease at her words. At this the Hu merchant turned to look at Su He as well. "You're a Shu brocade weaver, my lady? Do come closer and have a proper look, if you would."

Su He didn't hesitate for a moment, walking straight over to the two men, with Du Cheng following close behind, while the Hu woman too turned her attention to what was unfolding.

Su He examined it closely, then said, "The turns in a genuine Shu brocade pattern ought to be rounder, more refined — not like this, where the edges of the pattern aren't crisp enough. If I might touch the cloth, I could confirm it fully."

"Please, go ahead," said the Hu merchant.

The Hu woman brought over a basin of water for Su He to wash her hands. In that brief lull, Du Cheng noticed the flicker of panic in the merchant's eyes, and asked offhandedly, "And where might you have bought this Shu brocade, sir — which weaving house or shop in Jinguan City? The two of us are from Jinguan City ourselves, and have spent years weaving and trading brocade there. Even if we don't know every person or shop connected with Shu brocade in Jinguan City, we've likely heard something of most."

"My goods come from another Hu merchant. Who says I have to have gone to Jinguan City myself?" The merchant's voice noticeably dropped, and his eyes darted about.

"Don't let him ramble on — never been to Jinguan City, has he? Sounds to me like he's passing off fish eyes as pearls, palming off shoddy goods as the real thing." Su He dried her hands, waving off Du Cheng's need to bother with him further, then reached out and rubbed the cloth gently between her fingers — and that was all it took for her to know, one way or the other.

"Too thick, not soft enough. The thread's been twisted too tight — it feels completely different. This isn't Shu brocade." Su He withdrew her hand, perfectly composed.

"You—" The Hu merchant's face darkened with fury, and he rolled up the bolt of cloth and flung it back at the traveling merchant. The merchant tried to protest, but the Hu merchant cut him off. "Passing off fake goods, just as I thought — now get out of here with your counterfeits, before I have you hauled before the magistrate!"

The merchant, growing angry himself, shouted back, "Where'd this rough country girl come from? Whatever she says, you just believe it?" He turned to leave, only for the Hu merchant to seize him and haul him back.

"Take every last fake you ever sold me along with you, and return my money — or else we're going straight to the Chang'an county office and letting the magistrate settle who's right and who's wrong."

The merchant had no way out now, unable to leave even if he wanted to. Only once the Hu merchant had his men haul every one of the dozen-odd bolts of "Shu brocade" up from the cellar, and the "rough country girl" had shaken her head to reject every single one, did the merchant finally realize the game was up. He had no choice but to send a servant to fetch the money, load the dozen-odd bolts of "Shu brocade" back onto his cart, and slink off in disgrace — narrowly escaping what might have become a real catastrophe out of nowhere.
