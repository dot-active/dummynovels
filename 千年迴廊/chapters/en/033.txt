Chapter Eight: Settling the Score

Ever since Du Cheng had seen that letter home, polished by Bai Jue's own hand, a nagging unease had settled over him. It wasn't just that Su He couldn't write with such flourish — he knew her handwriting better than anyone, and it was certainly nothing so neat and elegant. As for Kangnawu and his wife, that was even less likely — a pair of Sogdians surely couldn't produce something like that either. So his chest tightened with frustration, and he could hardly wait to race straight to Chang'an and get to the bottom of it, growing more sullen by the day.

The whole way from Jinguan City, racing hard toward Chang'an, everyone in the caravan could sense something was off with their young master, though no one dared ask about it aloud. There was no rain on this trip, but inside Du Cheng's chest, the wind howled and the rain lashed all the same. He wore a scowl the entire way, and whoever spoke up first risked becoming the unlucky target of his temper. Though their young master's temperament was ordinarily mild enough, when the master of the house wore a face like thunder, no one had the nerve to say a single extra word.

The moment they arrived in Chang'an, Du Cheng, knowing Su He had been running off to the spice shop again, did something unprecedented — the very instant the goods were unloaded, he had the others tally the inventory and store the Shu brocade away, while he himself rushed off toward the spice shop, carrying the fifteen bolts Kangnawu had ordered. After handing the goods over to Kangnawu, he hastily pulled Su He outside the shop and demanded, without a shred of courtesy, "Which man wrote that letter for you?"

"Twentieth Young Master Bai — why do you ask?" Su He looked utterly baffled.

"Who is this Twentieth Young Master Bai? What's your relation to him, that he'd write a letter home on your behalf?"

"A scholar I met at the Hu tavern, heading to the capital for the examinations. He happened to be in the shop buying spices, and seeing that I couldn't get a single word down, he was kind enough to help me polish it a bit. What's gotten into you? We haven't seen each other in ages, and instead of a single kind word, you show up and interrogate me like I'm on trial?"

"I told you not to go to that Hu tavern, and you went anyway?" This only stoked Du Cheng's temper further — Su He's blatant, deliberate disregard for his warning was almost too much to bear.

"You clearly said no young lady should go in alone — wasn't Uncle Nawu and Miwei there with me the whole time? Why is it you're allowed to go look at Hu hostesses, but I'm not? You've clearly got a guilty conscience — with sights that pretty, not looking would be the real surprise. You're just trying to fool me."

The two of them argued loudly right outside, and passersby couldn't help glancing their way. Du Cheng lowered his voice. "She dances her dance, I mind my business talk — don't go accusing an innocent man."

"Is it that you don't trust me, or that you don't trust yourself? As for me — I trust myself just fine." Su He, thoroughly annoyed, gave a huff and turned to march back into the spice shop.

"I... of course I trust you. It's that Twentieth Young Master Bai I don't trust — showing kindness for no reason at all, who knows what he's really after? Shouldn't you keep your distance from him?" Du Cheng was already beginning to show signs of "total collapse."

"How am I supposed to keep my distance? Am I meant to flee the moment he sets foot in the shop? I've done nothing wrong, so why should I fear him? You're being completely unreasonable."

At this point Kangnawu, seeing both of them still worked up, stepped in to placate Du Cheng. "Don't fret, Fifth Young Master — A-He is sharp as a tack, no telling who'd come out worse in this. My wife and I could see it clear as day — you know better than anyone who could possibly match your A-He." Then he related, in full detail, Su He's "heartfelt gratitude" toward Bai Jue, and at that Du Cheng couldn't help laughing — his own strategist wife-to-be certainly lived up to her reputation. In truth, he was something of a "victim" himself in all this, and Kangnawu's remark — "you know better than anyone who could possibly match your A-He" — struck rather close to home for him.

And just like that, Du Cheng's anger dissolved entirely. It had only ever been jealousy talking — how could he truly hold it against Su He? So he reached out to catch her sleeve, telling her not to hold it against him, that every fault under heaven was his own, and begging her, as the better and bigger person, not to trouble herself over a lesser man's mistakes.

Su He knocked his hand away, still not entirely appeased. She pointed at her own hair bun and said, displeased, "Do you see this? What is it?"

Du Cheng followed her finger and saw, tucked into her hair, the very same small comb he'd given her days before. At this Du Cheng was thoroughly "routed," and could only mumble meekly, "I know... I know. It's all my fault."

Su He gave a huff and, "pressing her advantage," said, "The comb sits in my hair, but where does the person sit in your heart? Just look at how you've treated me."

The shopgirl, who hadn't dared make a sound since her master had come "storming" into the spice shop, now saw that the outcome was clear and the victor decided, and finally worked up the nerve to sidle over and murmur gently to Su He.

"Don't be angry, Miss. Master's already admitted his fault — and he only ever admits fault to you, never to any of us. That just proves how much you mean to him."

Even Su He found herself defeated by the shopgirl's words — since when did a master ever apologize to a hired hand? She couldn't help but burst out laughing despite herself.

"Miss is laughing now... she's not angry anymore... Master might just be saved after all..."
