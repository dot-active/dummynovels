Chapter Two: Caught in the Rain

Ten days after the caravan left Jinguan City, the sky seemed to sour, its face darkening as clouds sank lower and lower in heavy layers. Du Cheng could tell a downpour was coming, and fearing the wind and rain might turn fierce, he had the whole caravan stop to check that the ropes and oilcloth wrappings were all secure. He told Su He to sit tight in the carriage, so she wouldn't be caught in the wind and rain.

After thinking it over a moment, Du Cheng gave the order to add another layer of waterproof wrapping and retighten all the ropes. Watching everyone hard at work, Su He couldn't see any reason she should sit hiding in the carriage — so she jumped down too, wanting to do her part. She knew it was heavy, rough labor, but surely she could lend a small hand at least; whatever else she was, she wasn't the sort to sit idle while others worked.

Du Cheng caught sight of Su He running over, but he had no time to spare to send her back to the carriage — the rain was nearly upon them, and the work at hand couldn't wait. He knew Su He's temperament all too well besides; she was no delicate, fragile flower, but a girl who had always had a mind of her own since childhood, so he let her be.

No sooner had he thought this than the rain came crashing down without mercy. Su He climbed up onto the crates to help press the ropes tight, her slender hands looking especially delicate against the pounding rain, yet she never once thought of backing away. Only now did she understand — Du Cheng's road wasn't just long and grueling; it was also plagued, again and again, by unpredictable turns of weather. This work was truly harsh beyond anything — far more exhausting, at least, than she had ever imagined. In the workshop, what did she know of being battered by sun and rain? Without quite meaning to, her heart ached for A-Cheng, and for the whole caravan that traveled with him.

Du Cheng had followed his father transporting brocade since he was fourteen, and had taken over the trade entirely by the time he was sixteen. These days his father rarely made the journey himself anymore, unless some important deal in Chang'an truly required his personal attention.

Watching Du Cheng in the driving rain, Su He's eyes grew hot and damp, but mixed in with the rain streaming down her face, no one could have told the tears from the rainwater. Du Cheng directed everyone with steady composure, and the whole caravan worked as one — and thanks to their careful protection, the Shu brocade, woven thread by painstaking thread back in the workshop, came through without the slightest damage from the rain.

Once the reinforcing work was done, Du Cheng pulled Su He back into the carriage, fetched a dry cloth, and set about wiping down her face and hair. Fortunately, Su He had dressed as a man for ease of travel, so her hair carried none of the elaborate ornaments it usually did — only the small comb Du Cheng had brought back from Chang'an. She simply used it to comb out both her own soaked hair and his.

"We'll need to find an inn to stay at tonight, and get properly washed up," Du Cheng said, still wiping at Su He's hair.

"Is the inn far?" Su He had fetched a dry cloth of her own too, and gestured for Du Cheng to crouch down lower so she could wipe his face and hair in turn.

"Not far." Du Cheng crouched down, comforting her. "In a bit I'll turn my back so you can change your clothes, then wrap your hair in a dry cloth. We'll press on a little, and the moment we reach the inn, I'll have them heat water for you to bathe first thing."

"All right." Su He nodded, smiling. "You get your own clothes ready first — I'm quick with my hands. Once I'm changed, you hurry and change too."

"All right." Du Cheng nimbly dug out a set of his own clothes, then turned away.

"Do the others have dry cloths to use too?" Su He asked as she changed.

"Don't worry, everyone thought of that — they all prepared for it before we set out."

Hearing Du Cheng's answer, Su He suddenly felt a solid, steady sense of reassurance settle over her. Back in Jinguan City, she'd always thought of him as a bit mischievous now and then; but traveling this road with him, she was only now discovering how steady and dependable Du Cheng truly was — quite unlike how he seemed at home.

"A-Cheng, you've had a hard time of it," Su He said. Soaked through as she was, her heart felt warm all the same.

"For the sake of both our families, some hardship is worth bearing. I'd hoped to shield you from the wind and rain, but instead you've come to suffer alongside me — it weighs on my conscience..."

"It was my own choice to come... and besides, now that I know how hard this is for you, I'll be good to you for it, from now on."
