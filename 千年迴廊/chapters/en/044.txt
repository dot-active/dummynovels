Chapter Seven: Warm Hospitality

For several days in a row, apart from the first day when Wu Jingyu led Zhu Changqing on a rough tour of Jingdezhen, Zhu Changqing took Li Zong and wandered about on his own, taking his midday meals in taverns, so as not to trouble the Wu residence again and so as to see how the taverns were faring.

Taverns were crowded, with all sorts of people mixed together. Listening to the noise at each table, people of different stations talking of different topics and chatting freely about everything under the sun was the best way for Zhu Changqing to "take in the people's conditions." Sometimes the shopkeeper would chat a bit with the customers, and the scene became livelier still. Zhu Changqing was mostly a calm listener who rarely interjected, only occasionally saying a few words when a topic interested him. As he came more often, the shopkeeper and waiters gradually took note of him, finding him refined and gentle, with a natural air of nobility. Thinking of the commotion in front of the Wu porcelain shop a few days earlier, they couldn't help guessing that he must be Heir Zhu Changqing of Huai. After some discussion and deliberation, the shopkeeper finally stepped forward to ask.

"Sir, are you Your Highness the Heir of Huai?"

This question from the shopkeeper instantly caused an uproar in the tavern, and everyone turned to look at Zhu Changqing as one.

Before Zhu Changqing could say a word, someone in the crowd cried out, "I've seen him! He came to the warehouse and watched us bring goods in and out."

"I've seen him too, he was talking to the boatmen at the wharf."

The owner of the pastry shop suddenly came to himself as well, and said excitedly, "He bought osmanthus cake at my shop just yesterday!"

Just then a porcelain merchant walked in from outside. He had just finished a big deal with the Wu porcelain shop and had turned to the tavern first to appease his belly. He had barely stepped through the door when he caught sight of Zhu Changqing, and rushed over like the wind, dropping to his knees and bowing low. "This humble man pays respects to Your Highness the Heir of Huai." At once the whole tavern knelt with thuds and thumps, cries of "This humble man pays respects to Your Highness the Heir of Huai" rising here and there, mingled with the knocking of tables and chairs, until even Zhu Changqing was flustered.

"Please... please, all of you, rise!" Zhu Changqing sprang to his feet.

Li Zong nearly burst out laughing at the sight. His Heir was so flustered he stammered, after all, he was only seventeen or eighteen, and at Jingdezhen his youthful callowness had finally been able to "show." So, holding back his laughter, he helped out. "The Heir is so startled he can't even sit still. Why don't you all get up quickly?"

Hearing this, the crowd rose one after another to look at Zhu Changqing. Those nearest hurried to pull a bench over for him and soothed him: "The Heir is awfully timid! Isn't being bowed to by subjects something you're used to? Sit down and be at ease. We're all honest folk, not bad men. Don't be afraid."

Seeing Zhu Changqing's delicate good looks and his age of seventeen or eighteen, they naturally treated him as warmly as one of their own children.

"Where is the Heir staying in Jingdezhen?"

Before Zhu Changqing could react, the merchant who had knelt first eagerly answered, "At the Wus', in the garden residence of the Wu porcelain shop, being well looked after..."

"Since it's the Heir, this meal is on the house," the tavern shopkeeper said without the slightest hesitation, immediately joining the ranks of those "looking after him well."

"And my shop too... Come sit there later, Heir, and let this old man do his part as host. There are cakes of every kind with all the color, aroma and flavor you could want, to choose as you please, and a little tea, so that my humble shop can share some of the glory too," Boss Jiang of the pastry shop said, doing all he could to "drum up business." Basically they were so enthusiastic that they gave Zhu Changqing no chance to react; with one saying this and another saying that, nearly everyone jumped out to invite the Heir to "grace our humble homes," as if having the Heir sit in their houses were an honor to the ancestors.

"How... can this be allowed?" Zhu Changqing was stunned. He looked at the crowd, then at Li Zong, and couldn't help blushing with embarrassment. What kind of "not disturbing the people" was this? He had unwittingly "stirred a pond of spring water," and it was awkward indeed.

"It can, it can." The owner of the candied-fruit shop joined the camp too, and seeing the Heir blush made him all the more eager. "In my shop the sugared plums, peaches, apricots, kumquats... are all delicious, truly the best tea snacks in all of Jingdezhen."

"You're boasting, aren't you?" Boss Jiang, not to be outdone, said, "My cakes are the true top quality."

As a result, when he returned to the Wu garden residence that evening, Li Zong's hands were full of the townspeople's "sincere tribute": things to eat, to drink, to use... everything one could name. Li Zong's two hands weren't enough, and Zhu Changqing helped carry some too, a sight that left Wu Jingyu utterly dumbfounded.
