Cultural Notes

1. Blue-and-White Porcelain:
Blue-and-white porcelain is an underglaze-decorated ware in which designs are painted on the porcelain body with cobalt-containing pigment, then covered with a transparent glaze and fired at high temperature, producing blue patterns. Early blue-and-white existed in the Tang dynasty and gradually matured in the Yuan; in the Ming, the firing technology of Jingdezhen's blue-and-white grew ever more refined, becoming an important representative of Chinese porcelain and being exported abroad in large quantities.

2. The Succession Dispute:
Zhu Yiju, Prince of Huai, doted on Wang Aier and her son Zhu Changhong, and tried to replace the legitimate eldest son Zhu Changqing's right of succession with the son of a concubine, giving rise to the succession dispute in the Huai princely household. The affair later alarmed the court, and in the end Zhu Changqing's position as legitimate heir was preserved.

3. Jing'e Academy:
After Zhu Changqing, Prince of Huai, inherited the title, he founded the Jing'e Academy in Raozhou, inviting Pan Yi'e, the princely household's chief administrator, to lecture, and providing a place of study for children of the imperial clan and talented local youths. The Gazetteer of Poyang County records that Zhu Changqing was "supremely filial in nature, close to the worthy and delighting in goodness," and also records his founding of the academy and engaging of teachers.

4. Zhu Changqing's Rescue of Zhang Yunlun:
In the last years of Chongzhen, the Commander-in-Chief Yang Sichang demanded gold from Raozhou to reward the troops. Zhang Yunlun, the prefect of Raozhou, cared for the people and pleaded on their behalf, and nearly brought disaster on himself. The Prince of Huai, Zhu Changqing, on learning of this, sent men with gold to relieve the situation, sparing Zhang Yunlun from calamity and at the same time lifting from the people of Raozhou the burden of forcibly levied military funds. This is recorded in the Gazetteer of Raozhou Prefecture and the Gazetteer of Poyang County.

5. Failed Uprising and Withdrawal to Jingdezhen:
In 1645, after the fall of the Southern Ming Hongguang regime, Zhu Changqing, Prince of Huai, raised troops in an attempt to restore the Ming house, but was soon plundered by soldiers from Leping and withdrew to live in Jingdezhen, and Raozhou subsequently fell as well. This is recorded in Xu Zi's Xiaotian Jizhuan: Buyi, whose original text reads: "In the yiyou year, when the Southern Capital fell, he raised troops to plan a restoration; within months he was plundered by Leping soldiers and withdrew to live in Jingdezhen, and Raozhou was lost as well."

6. Joining the Longwu Regime:
In 1645, Zhu Yujian, Prince of Tang, took the throne in Fuzhou and founded the Longwu regime. After his failed uprising in Jiangxi, Zhu Changqing, Prince of Huai, went south to Fujian and to Fuzhou to join the Longwu Emperor. In 1646 the Longwu regime fell, and Zhu Changqing fled south to Guangzhou with the other princes.

7. Regent of the Realm:
In the first month of 1648 (the second year of Yongli), Zheng Chenggong, not knowing the Yongli Emperor Zhu Youlang had already taken the throne, installed Zhu Changqing, Prince of Huai, as Regent of the Realm in his army and changed the era name to "Dongwu." Later, learning that the Yongli Emperor still lived, Zheng Chenggong sent an envoy presenting a memorial declaring allegiance. In the tenth month of the same year, the Yongli Emperor sent an envoy to enfeoff Zheng Chenggong as Marquis Weiyuan, and Zheng Chenggong followed the Yongli calendar, whereupon Zhu Changqing stepped down as regent. This is recorded in vol. 7 of the Xu Ming Ji Shi Ben Mo.
