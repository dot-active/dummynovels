Chapter Thirteen: Fuzhou

After crossing mountains and rivers through great hardship, the party finally reached Fuzhou. Thanks to the protection of the dozen or so sworn retainers under Zhu Changqing's command, and to Wu Jingyu's careful planning and handling of their money and goods, they met with no great trouble along the way. Though Zhu Changqing had an innate nobility, once dressed in plain cloth he was not so conspicuous, and mixed among the crowd he went unrecognized, which was truly Wu Jingyu's doing.

The Longwu Emperor, knowing the Prince of Huai had arrived in Fuzhou, welcomed him warmly and specially granted a Huai princely residence. But the Longwu court's finances were also extremely strained, and it was impossible for it to match the former standard of a prince's household; even the Longwu Emperor himself economized on clothing and food to avoid extravagance, let alone the other princes. As for Wu Jingyu's family, having just arrived with no place to stay, they lodged for the time being in the Huai princely residence. By day she followed brokers to look at houses for rent, and also had to call on the Wu family's merchant contacts in Fuzhou and get to know local porcelain dealers, so that the days since Jingyu came to Fuzhou were so busy her feet barely touched the ground, and she spun like a top without stopping.

One evening, Zhi'er was keeping Wu Jingyu company on a stone seat in the pavilion of the palace garden, pounding the mistress's sore feet after her running about for most of the day. Zhu Changqing happened to walk over and, seeing this, his heart filled with pity. He was a prince of standing, who ought to shelter the woman he loved, yet all along the road it had been Jingyu who looked after him. So he went and sat beside Wu Jingyu and said, "Let me do it instead." And he reached out with both hands, lifted Jingyu's feet onto himself, and began kneading and pounding them for her.

"Your Highness's body is worth a thousand gold. How can you..." Wu Jingyu was startled and for a moment could not react.

"Don't say that this prince of mine is a prince in name only now, with nothing left. Even if everything were as usual, wouldn't it be only right to rub the tired feet of the woman I love?"

Seeing this, Zhi'er tactfully withdrew far away; Li Zong, who followed Zhu Changqing, also stepped back.

"Since my youth I have wanted to give you a rank and name. Back then you had the Wu family business to keep, and I had my duties as a prince of the clan. Now that all has changed with time, and the obstacles then are gone, the Ming's institutions still stand. Now I can give you no title at all, yet thanks to your support and help all along the way, seeing you run about so, unable to do it for you, I only rub your legs a little. Would you not even grant me this chance?" Zhu Changqing said apologetically as he kneaded Wu Jingyu's feet.

Wu Jingyu looked at Zhu Changqing for a long moment, and at last nodded with a smile. "Jingyu is the Great Head of the Wu family. Never mind that the Prince was coming to Fuzhou; Jingyu was already planning to move south, and thanks to the Prince's protection along the way, my family was able to reach Fuzhou safely. Even if times change and seas turn to mulberry fields, so long as true feeling remains, it is the most precious priceless treasure in these troubled times, and nothing else matters."

Hearing this, Zhu Changqing's tightly knit brow eased somewhat. He gave a bitter smile and said lightly, "How long Fuzhou can hold is hard to say. The Qing army advances like a bamboo splitting, and that is why you only rent a house and buy no property. Remember this: at the slightest stir, you must withdraw as fast as you can; if anything unforeseen should befall me, you must not turn back and must not look for me..."

Hearing Zhu Changqing's words, Wu Jingyu felt tears fill her eyes. It was true that he regretted being unable to give her a title as his wife, and truer still that he did not wish to drag her down when disaster came. For a prince who would never surrender, the ending was almost only one: succeed, or die for the cause.

So Wu Jingyu wept.

"I won't allow Your Highness to say that..."

When Wu Jingyu wept, Zhu Changqing shed tears too. He drew Jingyu into his arms and patted her gently. "This is the fate of a prince of the clan; you must keep it clear in your heart."

"No..." Jingyu buried her face in Zhu Changqing's chest and wept uncontrollably.

"Do you remember Yu Qian, whom I once spoke of to you?" Zhu Changqing said quietly. "In the end he was framed and killed. Holding fast to integrity must be paid for..."

By now Jingyu was sobbing helplessly, holding Zhu Changqing tightly. Even though Zhi'er learned that supper was ready, she dared not interrupt the two.

Note: Marriages of princes of the Ming imperial clan were bound by the court's ritual regulations; a prince could not freely take or enthrone a consort. According to the Collected Statutes of the Great Ming, vol. 57, "Rites of the Princely Houses III: Marriage," it was decided in the tenth year of Wanli (1582) that when the consort of a prince or commandery prince died without heirs, a successor consort could be requested; if he already had heirs, whether legitimate or illegitimate, no successor consort could be selected, and concubines could only be arranged under the "inner assistant" system to manage the household and rear the children, and a secondary consort could not be petitioned for either.
