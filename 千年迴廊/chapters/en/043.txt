Chapter Six: Keeping Company

After the midday meal, Zhu Changqing could not stop praising it. Setting aside the grandeur of the setting, it was no way inferior to the princely palace, and a famous chef's hand truly made a difference.

After the meal, the two decided to walk off the food and strolled back to the porcelain shop, which was not far away. Zhu Changqing brought Li Zong, and Wu Jingyu brought Zhi'er, two in front and two behind, the four of them walking together.

Behind the porcelain shop lay several connected courtyards, and one of them was the Wu family's workshop. Even before they drew near, they could hear the hubbub of voices inside. Jingyu led Zhu Changqing in, and they saw the craftsmen each at their own task: some kneading clay, some throwing vessels on the wheel, some trimming the shaped bodies neatly; at the other end, a few painters bent over their desks, brushes in hand, tracing designs stroke by stroke on plain white porcelain bodies.

Zhu Changqing looked carefully all along the way, and paused for a while beside the painters in particular. He saw that the patterns on the bodies were a dull gray-black, quite unlike the blue-and-white porcelain he had seen in the shop, and couldn't help asking, "Is this the blue-and-white before it goes into the kiln?"

"It is," Wu Jingyu answered with a smile. "It looks unremarkable now, but only after it is glazed and fired in the kiln will it show its blue color."

Further in were the places for glazing and for loading saggars. As Jingyu walked, she briefly explained each step of the process, and though Zhu Changqing knew nothing of porcelain-making, he listened with relish. Only when they had gone all the way around did he learn that an ordinary-looking cup, plate, bowl or saucer in the shop had to pass through so many hands, from a lump of porcelain clay to coming out of the kiln fired.

"No wonder Jingdezhen can support so many people on porcelain alone," Zhu Changqing said thoughtfully.

"Didn't the Heir tell that ruffian, Young Master Zhang, 'what one person uses is made ready by the work of a hundred artisans'?"

"This is what is called 'seeing is believing'; what's in books and what one hears is all empty." Zhu Changqing's curious and clear eyes reflected the many trades at work, and a feeling of respect rose in his heart.

"That's because the Heir is unlike other imperial descendants. In this world, even a mere rich-family son like Young Master Zhang can be raised into something like a street ruffian, never mind any scion of royalty. I suppose the Princess Consort of Huai must be a virtuous and gentle woman, to have brought up the Heir, a 'Zhu family member,' so mild and honest." Watching Zhu Changqing's earnest look, Wu Jingyu couldn't help teasing him about being a "Zhu family member" again.

"You're scolding people in roundabout ways again?" Zhu Changqing was a little helpless, and glanced at Wu Jingyu. Seeing her cover her mouth and laugh lightly, her clever eyes and brows seemed to gather into a touch of mischief, lively and leaping beneath her brisk temperament.

"I'm praising the Princess Consort..." Wu Jingyu burst into laughter herself, her faintly flushed cheeks looking bright and charming. Zhu Changqing had meant to say something, but for a moment couldn't get it out, and only gazed at Jingyu in a daze.

"Miss, don't be naughty. If Master hears of it, he'll scold you again," Zhi'er reminded Wu Jingyu from the side. The one she was being naughty with was the Heir.

"Don't you tell. My father always teaches me to speak and act like an old person, and I'm not even seventeen yet! It's truly asking too much." Pouting as she spoke, Wu Jingyu warned Zhi'er, "Don't you go behind my back and 'file a report on me' with my father. If you dare, tomorrow I'll send you to the kitchen to wash vegetables and cook rice, and you won't follow me anymore."

"My dear miss, how would Zhi'er dare wag her tongue before Master? But with so many eyes in the workshop watching, someone might well go and tell, so you mustn't blame Zhi'er unjustly."

"Go to the kitchen and have them make sweet lotus seed soup, plenty of it, enough that not only the Heir but everyone here gets a share. Whoever dares to wag their tongue after drinking the sweet soup won't even get cooling tea from now on."

"Thank you, miss." Hearing this, the crowd erupted in cheers, exchanged glances, and with perfect understanding declared aloud together, "We saw nothing and heard nothing."

For a moment the whole place was lively and full of energy. Zhu Changqing watched in a daze. Under Wu Jingyu's lead, the Wu workshop and porcelain shop were truly thriving, the whole place filled with harmony, and it made him think of the coldness of the princely palace. Mother Consort was dignified and virtuous, and would never lower herself to compete for favor with the tricks of Wang Aier, a woman of courtesan origin. For that reason, she and he had long been out of favor with Father Prince, which made the air there particularly stifling. Today, seeing such an amusing and warm scene, he could only gaze at Wu Jingyu in amazement, the corners of his mouth lifting unconsciously into a faint curve.

Beside him Li Zong smiled too. Looking back, he saw that Zhu Changqing's gaze had never left Wu Jingyu, and that a smile showed on his face. He seemed to have sensed something, but it wasn't for him to say it aloud.

At any rate, this was not the Heir he knew.
