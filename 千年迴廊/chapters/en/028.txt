Chapter Three: The West Market

Travel-worn and dust-covered after more than a month on the road, Su He finally set foot in the Chang'an she had longed for so eagerly. The wards and markets here were laid out neat and orderly, like a chessboard — nothing at all like Jinguan City, threaded through with rivers and canals, its greater and lesser walled districts joined together. Su He sat in the carriage craning her neck this way and that, her eyes hardly able to keep up with it all.

At last, after Du Cheng finished unloading, tallying, and carefully inspecting every last bolt of Shu brocade before storing it away, Su He could no longer wait to go browse the West Market, where Du Cheng had once bought her small comb. Far from looking worn out, she seemed positively glowing with energy, and Du Cheng couldn't help but marvel at it. Luckily he himself was only eighteen and young enough to keep up, and seeing how eager Su He was, he took her by the hand and led her out into the streets. Behind them came the shop hands, calling out cheerfully, "Take care, Young Master, Young Lady!" Every one of them was sharp enough to know exactly who Su He was to him, and hearing it said so openly left Du Cheng's heart blooming with joy, sweeping away what little tiredness remained in an instant.

The West Market had every novelty imaginable, and no shortage of shops run by Hu merchants besides, selling goods brought in from all across the Western Regions by way of the Silk Road — spices, medicinal herbs, felt rugs, jewelry, anything one could think of. Suddenly a warm scent of baked wheat drifted past, and Su He followed her nose until she spotted someone baking hu bing flatbreads in a clay oven nearby. As luck would have it, a fresh batch had just come out of the oven, and the smell alone was enough to set Su He's mouth watering.

"Two, please," Su He said, already pulling out her own purse to pay. But before she could, Du Cheng had already handed over two coins to the baker, gesturing for her to put her purse away.

"I'm right here with you — let me pay. Save your own travel money, in case you need it later."

Su He thought there was some sense in that, and didn't argue further. Besides, two flatbreads cost hardly anything, and there was no harm in letting A-Cheng play the generous host for once.

As Su He looked eagerly this way and that along the road, her eye caught a Hu tavern, lively music with an exotic, foreign flavor spilling out from within. Inside, blue-eyed Hu hostesses danced with bewitching grace, their movements enchanting, while a chorus of cheers and clapping rang out below — a lively scene indeed. Without a second thought, Su He lifted her foot to head straight in, but Du Cheng, seeing this, quickly pulled her back.

"All sorts wander in and out of that place — it's no place for a young lady like you."

Blocked this way, Su He felt a flicker of annoyance, and watching the Hu hostesses dance so gracefully within, along with all the patrons drinking and enjoying the show, she only grew more indignant.

"Aren't you right here with me? Besides, no one said a young lady can't go in." Su He still tried to twist free of Du Cheng's "restraint," but his hand, toughened from years of hauling brocade, held firm no matter how she struggled. So she pouted and went on, "Don't tell me you've been inside before yourself? How else would you know all sorts wander in and out of there?"

Du Cheng hadn't expected his "strategist" of a wife-to-be to seize on a single remark and spin it, in the blink of an eye, right back around onto him — and for a moment he found himself entirely at a loss for words.

"I..." Du Cheng was an honest man through and through, incapable of lying, and could only stammer, "I've been... a few times... when talking business... A-He, it's different... it's no place for a young lady..."

Hearing this only made Su He angrier, and she tugged even harder, trying to pry his hand loose.

"So you're allowed to look at the Hu hostesses, and I'm not? Fine then — if they're so pretty, why not just marry one and look at her every day, and save yourself the cost of going to a tavern for drinks?"

"That's not... A-He... don't be unreasonable... I was talking business... not looking at Hu hostesses..." The more Du Cheng protested, the more flustered he grew, and without quite realizing it, his grip loosened just enough. Su He seized her chance and finally broke free of his "restraint," taking off at a run straight for the tavern.

"I'll just take one look — let's see whether the Hu hostesses are prettier, or I am!"

Seeing Su He dart off toward the tavern in a flash, Du Cheng hurried after her and once again "brought her to hand."

"A-He is the prettiest... no one can compare... I'll marry A-He."

At that, Su He burst out laughing. Du Cheng's words had made her suddenly abandon all "resistance." Now that he had said as much, going on fighting him would only make her look unreasonable. Besides, Du Cheng would be heading back to Jinguan City in a few days anyway — there was no rush to visit the Hu tavern right this moment. Wouldn't it be far easier to come back once he'd left, rather than having to "fight her way through" like this now?

"Oh, very well then. Have it your way."
