Chapter Six: The Tavern

Du Cheng rested in Chang'an for several days before finally bidding Su He a reluctant farewell to head back to Jinguan City. Before he left, he couldn't help but give her instruction after instruction, telling her to be careful in everything, and arranged for a sharp, capable young shopgirl from the household to attend to her. He reminded Su He that wherever she went, she should bring the girl along, and more importantly — that she should stay away from anywhere she oughtn't go.

In the days that followed, Su He took the shopgirl along to wander the West Market and the East Market, and whenever she grew tired of that, she would head over to Kangnawu's spice shop to chat with Miwei over cups of fermented milk. Life passed pleasantly enough, yet one regret nagged at her all the while. That Hu tavern was thronged with customers every single day, and she was consumed with curiosity about it, yet she kept Du Cheng's warning in mind and so the chance to see it for herself never came.

One day Su He finally couldn't help confiding to Miwei her long-held longing to see the Hu tavern. Miwei laughed — so this young lady all the way from Jinguan City had taken such a particular fancy to the Hu tavern? Being a Hu woman herself, she felt no curiosity about it at all, but seeing how eager Su He was, she called Kangnawu over and told him to escort the three young ladies — Hu and Han alike — into the tavern to broaden their horizons for once.

The whole party marched in together, and a scene mixing Hu and Han customers together was nothing unusual inside a tavern like this. Amid the bustling crowd, every sort of person imaginable was present; aside from Su He and the shopgirl, who kept glancing around with wary curiosity, everyone else seemed perfectly used to it all.

At the center of the stage, a Hu hostess moved with graceful, swaying steps, the pearls and jade rings and bangles on her body chiming with a clear, crisp sound as she danced. Amid the lively, spirited music, her thin gauze robes swirled and fluttered with each dizzying spin, leaving the two young women unable to look away, gasping in admiration now and then.

"That's called the Sogdian whirling dance," Miwei called out over the noise to Su He.

While the two young women watched, transfixed, Kangnawu had already ordered food — grape wine, roasted mutton, fermented milk, hu bing flatbread, dried fruit — one dish after another arriving at the table. The shopgirl, eating as she watched, murmured absentmindedly into Su He's ear, "No wonder all the men from the shops love the Hu tavern so much, and yet they won't let us..." Before she even finished, she realized she'd said something foolish. Their young master had given endless warnings before he left, forbidding the young lady from entering the Hu tavern — and naturally, "all the men from the shops" included her own young master too. Now here they were, not only having come in themselves, but she'd also gone and dragged her own master into it while trying to have it both ways. She clamped her mouth shut, mortified.

Su He shot her a glare, remembering that Du Cheng had been inside this very tavern before, and felt rather sour about it. And there he was, claiming he'd never looked at the Hu hostesses! With a sight this lovely, not looking would be the real surprise.

Su He silently resolved that the next time she saw Du Cheng, she wouldn't let him wriggle out of it with a few smooth words — at the very least she'd have to "interrogate him under duress" until he confessed the truth.

Just as her thoughts were running wild, from a distance, a scholar in white robes wove his way through the crowd toward Su He and her party. He had a fair, clean complexion and an air of gentle refinement. Reaching the table, he bowed deeply to all four of them and said, "This humble scholar is Bai Jue, courtesy name Yingze, twentieth in my generation. I intend to sit for the jinshi examination this year, and seeing that everyone here carries themselves with such remarkable bearing, I hoped I might make your acquaintance."

Kangnawu rose to return the courtesy, introducing himself in turn, and took the opportunity to mention that his spice shop carried a full range of goods at fair prices. But he noticed the scholar's gaze kept drifting toward Su He beside him — and shrewd as Kangnawu was, how could he fail to see the young scholar's true intentions? Still, it was only the polite formalities of social exchange, with a bit of business drumming up thrown in besides, so he let it pass.

Bai Jue smoothly called the innkeeper over for more grape wine, along with some ghee and milk curds, insisting on treating them all. He raised his cup in a toast, draining his own first as a gesture of respect. Kangnawu and Miwei both knew perfectly well that this toast was really meant for Su He — except Su He didn't drink wine at all, so Bai Jue's little scheme was entirely wasted. The husband and wife exchanged a knowing glance, silently amused. It was fortunate they had come along too, or Su He would clearly have found herself "surrounded by peril" on every side.

Once the four of them had eaten and drunk their fill, and Su He and the shopgirl felt they'd had their fill of excitement as well, they finally took their leave of Bai Jue and returned to the spice shop...
