Chapter Four: Moving In

Wu Jingyu took her maid Zhi'er and led Zhu Changqing to the Wu family's garden residence, while Li Zong went back first to the Yuelai Inn to check out. Zhu Changqing specially instructed Li Zong to pay the full room charge for the day, so as not to take advantage of the innkeeper. Once he had checked out and collected the luggage, an assistant from the porcelain shop would guide him, so that Li Zong could reach the garden residence and rejoin Zhu Changqing without trouble.

Walking into this tranquil garden residence, white walls and dark tiles set off tall horse-head gables, and beyond the courtyard gate lay a world of its own. A covered corridor passed through the courtyard, its floor paved with bluestone, slender bamboo growing along the wall, and several rooms lay half hidden among the flowers and trees. Going further into the depths of the courtyard, the eaves on all four sides arched inward and drooped, and a small skywell burst into Zhu Changqing's view.

When Wu Jingyu saw Zhu Changqing stop, she stopped too. His eyes were full of delight.

"How exquisite," Zhu Changqing couldn't help exclaiming. Then he caught sight of a parasol tree in the skywell, and his face lit up all at once.

"What a coincidence." Zhu Changqing looked at Wu Jingyu and said gaily, "The courtyard where I live also has a parasol tree."

"Such a coincidence? It seems Your Highness the Heir is very fond of parasol trees." Seeing Zhu Changqing so pleased, Wu Jingyu was happy too, a true case of host and guest enjoying themselves together.

"Yes." Zhu Changqing nodded. "The Classic of Poetry, 'Greater Odes: Juan'e,' says: 'The phoenix cries out, on that high ridge; the parasol tree grows, on that sunlit slope.' The ancients believed the parasol tree draws the phoenix, and that the phoenix will perch on nothing but a parasol tree. I have loved this allusion since I was small."

Wu Jingyu watched Zhu Changqing's glowing face, and she seemed to catch the happy mood as well. Had she not been certain he was the Heir of Huai, at this moment he was no more than a youth of seventeen or eighteen.

"The phoenix chooses a fine tree to perch in, and a worthy man chooses where to serve. In the Tumu Crisis, Emperor Yingzong was captured by the Oirat, and the court and the country were shaken. Some argued for moving the capital south, but Yu Qian overrode all objections and held the capital. Later, appointed Minister of War, he reorganized the troops, personally met Esen's main force in battle, closed the city gates, and swore to die rather than retreat, and in the end drove the Oirat back."

Wu Jingyu listened, spellbound. She had read the Classic of Poetry, but she was not familiar with Yu Qian. The Heir spoke with soaring spirit, and she listened with keen interest.

"A mere civil official, for the sake of the Zhu family's realm, was this valiant and alone. As a Zhu clan prince, how could I only enjoy the honor of my rank and forget the duty I bear?"

Wu Jingyu looked at the heroic, youthful Zhu Changqing. He was truly quite unlike the Zhu family members she knew of. Never mind the flaws in the character of the Prince of Huai, Zhu Yiju; even the present Emperor neglected state affairs and had not held court in decades. Which of the great merchants, with their ears open in every direction, did not know that? Even a prince held no political or military power, much less an Heir who had not yet inherited the title. Yet he had the breadth and bearing to hold the people's suffering to his heart and care for family and country, so that he and those other "Zhu clansmen" scarcely seemed to be of one family.

After grumbling to herself for a moment, Wu Jingyu thought the young Heir before her uncommonly bright, and said to Zhu Changqing with a smile, "If every member of the Zhu family were like the Heir, it would truly be a blessing for all the people under heaven."

Zhu Changqing was startled at first, then gave a soft laugh and sigh. "How is this praise for me? You're only finding a roundabout way of scolding people."

Wu Jingyu went on smiling. "And not only scolding people in roundabout ways, but also daring to whip the eldest son of the Zhang family who didn't know better!"

Zhu Changqing looked at Wu Jingyu before him. She was only sixteen or seventeen, yet she could already manage the Wu family's affairs in perfect order: from instantly deciding to clear a separate courtyard for his lodging, to teaching a lesson to the troublemaker who had come to cause trouble. Her brisk spirit was worlds apart from that of the average rich family's young lady.

"You are so bold as to scold an imperial kinsman? This is not a small matter like teaching Zhang Huan a lesson. One misstep, and your head could roll." Zhu Changqing wasn't angry at all, and only found the girl interesting, so he teased her.

"Didn't I dare speak only because I saw the Heir is broad-minded and loves the people like his own children? Had the Heir glared with furrowed brows, or been as absurd as that ruffian of the Zhang family, I wouldn't have said a word." Wu Jingyu sized up the situation and, though she knew Zhu Changqing was not angry, she knew to "quit while ahead."

"That ruffian of the Zhang family?" Zhu Changqing hadn't expected Wu Jingyu to describe Zhang Huan so, and couldn't help laughing out loud. He also knew she was lavishing praise on him just to "avoid danger." What a clever girl.

"What is it?" Wu Jingyu lifted her chin with a proud look. "To deal with a ruffian, you have to use a ruffian's methods."

Just then Li Zong came over, and from far off saw his Heir laughing merrily. He could no longer remember how long it had been since his Heir had laughed so openly. So this Miss Wu had such a talent for joking and could make the ever-melancholy Heir show his smile without reserve.

Li Zong couldn't bear to interrupt the hard-won joy of Zhu Changqing, and stood far outside the courtyard gate watching, not stepping up at once to pay his respects, until Master Wu and his wife arrived side by side, and only then followed them in.
