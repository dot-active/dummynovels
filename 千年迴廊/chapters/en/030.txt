Chapter Five: New Acquaintances

As the merchant fled with his tail between his legs, the Hu merchant and his wife turned to Su He and Du Cheng, thanking them again and again with heartfelt gratitude.

The Hu merchant's name was Kangnawu, and his wife was Miwei — both Sogdians. Their daughter, Kangnanaizi, had married into a powerful Sogdian merchant family, becoming the wife of Anmoruo, a man of considerable standing on the Silk Road who also served as a caravan's sabao. His caravan traveled constantly between east and west, able to supply his father-in-law's spice shop with goods from all corners, which was why Kangnawu's spice shop in the West Market of Chang'an had grown to such an impressive scale.

Half a year earlier, Anmoruo had finally secured the travel permits allowing him to carry a set quantity of Shu brocade westward. He had hoped his father-in-law might help him source some fine Shu brocade in Chang'an ahead of time.

Kangnanaizi, meanwhile, also ran a jewelry and jade shop in the West Market, and even dealt in white jade from Khotan; since she so often traveled the Silk Road alongside her husband, she had hired someone trustworthy to manage the shop in her absence.

To express his heartfelt gratitude to Su He and Du Cheng, Kangnawu presented them both with a small gift of rare, precious borneol camphor. In turn, they bought a quantity of benzoin and agarwood, and the couple charged them little more than cost for it. The four of them talked happily together for quite a while, covering all manner of subjects about Shu brocade back in Jinguan City. Kangnawu decided then and there to purchase fifteen bolts of Shu brocade from Du Cheng, to be delivered the next time he came to Chang'an.

Du Cheng did the sums in his head and judged it feasible, so he took on the order. Three or four months from now, besides bringing goods to Chang'an, he would also need to bring Su He back to Jinguan City — a task he was, of course, only too glad to take on. As long as he wrote a letter home and had it carried back ahead of him by a trustworthy caravan, so that his family could prepare these extra fifteen bolts in time, there ought to be no real trouble. And if there was still some small shortfall, he could always arrange to draw on stock from weaving households he knew and dealt with regularly.

"Once Anmoruo arrives in Chang'an in a little over four months and sees the Shu brocade for himself, perhaps we can settle matters for our continued partnership going forward," Kangnawu said with a smile.

After they had sat talking a good while, Miwei brought out a plate of wheat cakes baked crisp and fragrant, along with some dried fruit and grapes, and Su He ate with such evident delight that she couldn't stop praising it.

Miwei smiled and said that her daughter and Su He were close in age, and that she herself felt a special fondness for Su He too. She had only been in Chang'an three months herself, with no companions of her own age, and if Su He didn't mind, she was welcome to come by the shop often and keep her company.

* * *

The moment she got back to the Du family's shop, Su He hurried to send for a bronze mirror. Even Du Cheng was baffled by it. As soon as the mirror was brought, Su He had him hold it steady while she looked herself over from every angle, running a hand over her face, which had grown a little sun-darkened. Before, sitting at her loom day after day, she had hardly seen the sun at all, her skin fair and fine. But this trip to Chang'an had not only left her considerably more tanned — her skin had grown rougher too...

"So it's true — I really have gone all rough," Su He murmured.

The moment he heard Su He's complaint, Du Cheng inwardly groaned. That fellow selling fake Shu brocade earlier had called her "rough country girl" over and over, and it was plain the words had struck home.

"But we haven't even had a chance to wash up and fix ourselves yet," Du Cheng offered, weakly, trying to soothe her.

"Then wouldn't that make me uglier than those Hu hostesses right now? Didn't you say just now that I was the prettiest of all? Were you lying to me, then?"

Du Cheng was caught off guard — Su He's gift for turning the tables on him in an argument was truly formidable; she'd cornered him with just a few words, using his own weapon against him.

"That's..." Du Cheng's hand, holding the mirror, nearly wobbled, but he forced himself to steady it, scrambling to think of some way to salvage the situation.

"A-He is naturally beautiful — even sun-darkened, you're lovely, and it'll fade back in a day or two besides. And anyway, I never... I never looked at the Hu hostesses at all..."

"Never looked? Then how would you know I'm prettier than they are?"

"Because in my heart, there's only ever been A-He..."

At this, everyone in and around the shop pressed their lips together, trying not to laugh, and a few couldn't hold it in and burst out laughing openly. Their young master, usually so composed and methodical in everything he did, went utterly to pieces in front of Su He — the contrast was striking. Thinking it over carefully, they figured they'd better serve the young lady with all due diligence in the future, lest they end up faring even worse than their own young master someday.

Seeing everyone laughing, Su He finally let Du Cheng off the hook, and had water heated for a proper bath, determined to wash and tidy herself up thoroughly, and wash away this humiliation once and for all.

Note: Sabao, a transliteration from the Sogdian, meaning the leader of a caravan.
