Chapter Ten: The Fall of the Realm

In the third month of the seventeenth year of Chongzhen (1644), Li Zicheng captured Beijing, the Chongzhen Emperor, Zhu Youjian, died for his country, and the central government of the Ming dynasty fell. In the fifth month of the same year, Zhu Yousong, Prince of Fu, took the imperial throne in Nanjing and founded the first Southern Ming regime, with the reign title "Hongguang."

In the first year of Hongguang / second year of Shunzhi under the Qing (1645), Qing forces marched south and took Nanjing in the fifth month. The Hongguang Emperor fled, was soon captured, and his regime swiftly collapsed. As the Qing army pressed on, the Prince of Huai, Zhu Changqing, raised troops to resist the Qing, but within a few months he was plundered by soldiers from Leping, his situation collapsed, and he withdrew to reside in Jingdezhen.

Now, arriving in Jingdezhen once more, Zhu Changqing was past forty. Jingdezhen before his eyes, not yet ravaged by war, was as tranquil and harmonious as it had been twenty-odd years before. Accompanying the princely retinue, besides Li Zong, were a dozen or so sworn retainers, who had escorted Zhu Changqing all the way from the chaos to Jingdezhen.

Zhu Changqing again walked down from the busy wharf onto the riverside street, and everything that met his eyes was so familiar. Twenty years had passed, the realm had been overturned and smoke of war lay everywhere, but what had never changed was this great commercial town that he had never been able to forget in the depths of his memory.

That year he had left Jingdezhen cherishing the lovely hopes of youth, and the osmanthus cake in his hand had constantly reminded him of that lively, radiant Miss Wu. He had specially saved a few pieces to take back for Mother Consort, told her that he cared for Jingyu and wished to marry her as Heir's consort, and joyfully recounted all there was to say about Jingyu. Mother Consort, rarely seeing him beam so, sent a matchmaker to the Wu family to inquire about their wishes. But fate played its tricks. Jingyu had once had a younger brother, but after he died of smallpox at the age of six, Master Wu, overcome with grief, grew frail and ill, and the couple never had another child. Only Jingyu, their one daughter, remained, and she could only take a husband into the family to carry on the Wu line, and could not marry out. The Heir, as an imperial clan prince, could of course never marry into the Wu household, and so the bond between them was cut off.

Now setting foot on this land again, the scenery was as before but the people and matters had changed.

At this moment Zhu Changqing wore a black gauze winged cap and a red round-collared robe; the gold-woven coiled dragons on the chest, back and both shoulders proclaimed a prince's rank, but the jade belt at his waist had gathered a little dust. Days of rushing about had left the robe no longer as smooth as before, the hem stained with dirt, and the leather boots on his feet covered with the dust of the road. This princely everyday dress, together with a dozen followers, was indeed extremely apt to draw the stares of passersby.

Zhu Changqing halted before the Yuelai Inn, calming himself, wondering whether to lodge a dozen-odd people there. As he hesitated, a voice came from afar.

"Your... Your Highness?"

The voice was somewhat aged but very strong. Zhu Changqing looked and saw an old man whose hair was streaked with gray, leading a child of about five or six by the hand. His face was somewhat wrinkled, but the outline was vaguely familiar. The dozen-odd sworn retainers immediately raised their guard at the sound and closed around Zhu Changqing layer upon layer.

"Don't be alarmed, he's only an old acquaintance, a resident of the town," Zhu Changqing calmed his retainers, and signaled them to clear a path.

"Are you... Boss Jiang of the pastry shop?" Zhu Changqing stepped forward a few paces, hoping the man and the child would not be frightened by such a formation.

"Yes. Your Highness has a fine memory. Look, this commoner has grown old, and Your Highness can still recognize him..." Boss Jiang drew the child in front of him. "Quickly, pay your respects to His Highness."

The little child, somewhat ill at ease, still knelt and kowtowed to Zhu Changqing, and Boss Jiang knelt down together with him. Zhu Changqing immediately stepped forward and helped them up.

"Boss Jiang still thinks well of Changqing. The Emperor has died for the country, and the Prince of Fu, after taking the throne, was also captured by Qing forces; the Great Ming exists in name only, and the princes of the Zhu clan have become homeless dogs. How can I bear the bows of the common people?" Zhu Changqing's voice carried loneliness, and his expression showed even more sorrow.

"No, no..." Boss Jiang seemed somewhat choked. "You were the best of princes, who benefited the local people. With you in Raozhou, the people had schooling. When the court pressed hard for military funds, it was you who paid out silver to relieve everyone, and did not let that burden fall on the people again... The incompetent one was the Emperor, never Your Highness."

Tears glimmered in Zhu Changqing's eyes. With the country broken and the family lost, his uprising failed, he never imagined that the people of Jingdezhen would still acknowledge him as Prince of Huai.

"Is this... your grandson?"

"Yes. This commoner was about to take him to the wharf to see the boats. He loves watching boats most of all."

"How good." Zhu Changqing nodded, smiling through his tears. Fortunately, the fires of war had not yet reached here, the people's lives went on as usual, and Boss Jiang could still enjoy the pleasure of playing with his grandson. As for himself, he had set out on the road of rising against the Qing; his thirteen-year-old Heir, Zhu Yougui, had burned the Huai princely palace and fled south. The great house of Zhu had collapsed, the Huai palace was destroyed, and he had long had no home to return to. But seeing the people of Jingdezhen still at peace gave him a good deal of comfort inside.

"Will Your Highness stay in Jingdezhen for a while?" Boss Jiang, seeing him travel-worn and with a dozen-odd sworn retainers, thought that formerly the Prince of Huai had protected the people, and now the people should protect him. "This old man's household is large and the rooms are few. I'd be glad to invite Your Highness to stay at my home, but fear it would wrong Your Highness, being too crowded."

Zhu Changqing was about to say that having a place to stay was a gift from Heaven, but then reflected that it would disturb the Jiang family's life too much and cause them inconvenience, and felt it unwise. Boss Jiang then thought of the Wu family, the wealthiest in Jingdezhen, who also had a separate garden residence big enough to hold a dozen-odd people. Besides, more than twenty years ago the Wus had invited Zhu Changqing, then still Heir of Huai, to stay for ten days, so this might be a fitting expedient.

"This old man will go at once to talk it over with Madam Wu. Her garden residence is still empty, and has been kept up and repaired all along, not much different from twenty years ago."

At the mention of the garden residence and Madam Wu, Zhu Changqing's heart shook, but he strove to keep his face calm. Only Li Zong saw the signs, and in time answered for Zhu Changqing with, "We're obliged to you."

Watching Boss Jiang lead his grandson toward the Wu porcelain shop, memories of those days rose again in his heart.

More than twenty years. What would she look like now?

Note: According to the Collected Statutes of the Great Ming (Da Ming Huidian), "Rites of the Princely Houses III: Marriage," in the Ming dynasty princes and heirs had to choose consorts from respectable families, wedding them with proper rites and with court approval, and the choice was not limited to official families; commoner girls could also be selected.
