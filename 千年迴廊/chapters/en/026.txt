Chapter One: Faraway

Du Cheng walked into the Su family's weaving workshop, crossed through the main hall, and made his way straight to the weaving room, as familiar with the path as with his own home. One or two dozen looms were running at once, and even from a distance he could hear their clattering, back and forth, back and forth.

That sound was bound up tight with his own childhood memories. Whenever he was in Jinguan City, not a day went by without it filling his ears.

His family dealt in transporting and trading Shu brocade, and had long been neighbors with the Su family's weaving workshop. Besides their businesses both thriving, the two families got along warmly and helped each other at every turn, and over several generations had even tied themselves together through marriage.

Du Cheng was the eldest son of his household, though by the clan's generational ranking he was Fifth. He and Su He, the Su family's eldest daughter, had grown up playing together since childhood. He was three years older than her, and both sets of parents had long looked on with quiet hope of seeing the match come to fruition one day. Everyone knew it well, and teasing the two of them was practically a daily pastime.

"A-He! Here comes A-Cheng looking for you — put down your work already and go see your Young Master Du, won't you?"

"Oh, go on with you — aren't you afraid your tongues will slip from all that chattering?" Su He was never one to put up with this crowd of aunties and busybodies; her nature was frank and lively, and she had never had any patience for coy pretenses.

"A slipped tongue's no great matter — a sip of tea sets it right again." The women only laughed at her all the more for it.

Du Cheng stepped into the weaving room, greeted everyone politely, and then promptly pulled Su He back outside. Once they were somewhere private, he took out a small comb and said, "I bought this on my last trip, special, at the West Market in Chang'an — the latest fashion. Wear it, it'll suit you." So saying, he had Su He stand still while he tucked the comb into her hair bun with his own hands.

Du Cheng was, after all, still an eighteen-year-old man with clumsy fingers, and he ended up sticking the comb in crooked, which left Su He none too pleased. She fetched a bronze mirror and had Du Cheng hold it up, checked her reflection, and shot him a withering look for his trouble.

"I could've done it myself just fine. Now if they see this mess, they'll only laugh at me all over again."

Su He pouted, adjusting the comb in her own hair bun, muttering all the while, "When I was just born, someone said what a pity it was, a girl. But Father held my hand and said, proud as anything, 'A Su family girl is precious! Look at these long, slender fingers — she's bound to be a fine hand at weaving brocade one day.' But I don't want to spend my whole life sitting in front of a loom, weaving! I want to be like you, A-Cheng — I want to see Chang'an."

Du Cheng was struck speechless for a moment. From Jinguan City to Chang'an was a journey of a thousand li, over mountains and through passes — he had never once heard of a brocade-weaving girl with such "lofty ambitions." The one-way trip alone took at least a month or more, and now here was Su He, "struck by some wild fancy," leaving him quite thoroughly stunned.

"What foolishness is this you're spouting?" Du Cheng swallowed hard, several times, and added, "A young lady, making a journey that long — she'd come back all rough and weathered..."

"What does that matter? Once we get to Chang'an, I can just wash up and fix myself properly, can't I?" Su He waved a hand, gesturing for Du Cheng to lower the mirror, her expression perfectly at ease, as if this were nothing worth making a fuss over at all.

"Doesn't your family have a shop in Chang'an? It's not as though you'd be sending me off to wander alone out there. If you can go, why can't I come along and see the world too?"

"Would your father... really agree to it?" Du Cheng still looked utterly unconvinced.

"Who do you take me for?" Su He gave a sly little smile, and leaned in to whisper by Du Cheng's ear. "Of course Father would never agree, just like that. So I had Mother tell him this instead: 'Our daughter will be part of the Du family sooner or later anyway — it'll do her good to travel with the caravan once and learn more about their business. Traveling with Young Master Fifth, she'll have somewhere to stay in Chang'an too, and he'll see that she's well settled — there's no need for us to worry.' Father thought it over and decided it made sense, so he didn't object."

Du Cheng listened to all this, stunned into silence for a good while, then stuck up his thumb in praise. "All those years trading barbs with the aunties and young ladies weren't wasted on you at all! A mind this sharp, and you're not off serving as some general's strategist — it's really a waste, keeping you in Yizhou weaving brocade."

"Isn't that the truth?" Su He laughed again. "Next time you go to Chang'an, you'd better bring me along."
