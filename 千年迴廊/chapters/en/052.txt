Chapter Fifteen: The End of the Road

In the tenth month of 1648, the Yongli Emperor sent an envoy to Zheng Chenggong's army, conferring on Zheng Chenggong titles including Marquis Weiyuan and Grand General Who Subdues Rebels. Zheng Chenggong decided to follow the Yongli calendar instead, no longer using the Dongwu era name. Learning that the Yongli court still existed, Zhu Changqing, to prevent the Southern Ming regimes from each going its own way, stepped down from the position of regent, ending the Dongwu regime that had lasted about ten months.

One cold day, in the afternoon when the sun was good, Zhu Changqing's carriage stopped before Wu Jingyu's house. Li Zong and the sworn retainers carried him down from the carriage on their backs, set him on a sedan chair, and bore him into the Wu residence. He wore thick winter clothes with a fox-fur cloak besides, wrapped up tightly, yet he could still be seen shivering a little.

Wu Jingyu had long since lit a charcoal fire for Zhu Changqing to make the main hall warm and comfortable, and had told her son to carry in a reclining drunkard's chair, so that the gravely ill Zhu Changqing could lie half-reclined in the hall instead of sitting stiffly upright. She had also moved a tea table beside the chair, and on it set freshly brewed tea and a plate of osmanthus cake.

Wu Jingyu had received Li Zong's notice and knew that Zhu Changqing's illness had come on fiercely and was likely grave. Zhu Changqing had met yesterday with the Heir, his son Zhu Yougui, from whom he had long been separated, and it seemed to lift a stone from his heart, yet it was also like leaving a will: he had said many things about what should follow after his death to the Heir. At this point he wished the Heir to live well; so long as he did not surrender, living under a hidden name would not be a bad choice. Having left his instructions with the Heir, knowing he was safe and his family still alive, Zhu Changqing found he was most unable to let go of Wu Jingyu.

"You said it was just a chill. Why is it so serious?" Looking at Zhu Changqing's deathly pale face, Wu Jingyu's heart was heavy, but her face remained warm, forcing a calm.

"I feared you would worry, and told Li Zong not to tell the truth," Zhu Changqing said weakly, his speech slow.

"But... I must come... to see... you... one last time." He barely finished before breaking into gasps. Seeing this, Wu Jingyu hurriedly patted his chest to ease his breathing.

"Your Highness! I won't allow you to say that." Grief welled up in Wu Jingyu; though she had known in her heart that a "last time" was coming, hearing Zhu Changqing say it she could no longer hold back, and her voice choked.

"If I am gone, you and your... two sons will lose shelter... consider going... to... Kinmen," Zhu Changqing continued with great effort. "Across... the sea... the Qing... army... cannot... take it... for a time..."

"Your Highness..." Wu Jingyu, grief-stricken, gently laid her head on Zhu Changqing's chest, feeling his feeble breathing rising and falling, his breath disordered.

Zhu Changqing with difficulty stroked Wu Jingyu's hair. Besides the restoration of the Ming, Wu Jingyu was the greatest concern he could not let go.

"I'm... sorry... I can't... keep... accompanying... you."

Held limply in Zhu Changqing's arms, Wu Jingyu felt his arms loosen more and more as time passed. She wiped her tears and said softly, "Osmanthus cake can't be bought here. Hearing that Your Highness had no appetite for days, Jingyu and Zhi'er tried making it ourselves. Would Your Highness like a bite, to see if it has the taste of Jiang's osmanthus cake from back then?"

"Yes." A trace of a smile showed on Zhu Changqing's face as he waited for Wu Jingyu to pick up the osmanthus cake from the tea table beside him and gently place it in his mouth.

"It's made so well." Zhu Changqing took a bite and chewed with effort, and his face showed still more of a smile.

Wu Jingyu picked up the tea bowl and said, weeping as she spoke, "This is the last of the Songluo tea. Take a sip." She fed Zhu Changqing a mouthful.

"It's Jingyu..." Zhu Changqing was now barely breathing, his breath ever shallower, and at this moment all the things of the palace, all the things with Jingyu, and the parasol tree in the skywell came to his mind one by one...

"You are the best prince in Jingyu's heart, who worried for the country and the people and cared nothing for personal rank..." She held Zhu Changqing's hand tightly, and seeing a smile on his face, watched him peacefully complete his life.

Li Zong and the sworn retainers knelt one by one, sobbing.

In the winter of that year, the Prince of Huai, Zhu Changqing, passed away.
