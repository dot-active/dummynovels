Chapter 8: A Loving Mother After the Storm, Light Through the Cracks

    Time slipped by silently, and a year passed.

    In the An family's living room, the afternoon sunlight fell gently across the plush carpet. An Yilin sat quietly in the corner of the sofa, a half-read book held loosely in her hand, her expression calm. Over this past year, she had improved somewhat from before — at the very least, she would smile watching the twins now, and would gently tuck in their blankets when they were fast asleep.

    And yet, that pane of glass separating her from the world had never gone away. She seemed to be performing the duties of a mother, yet she had still never truly handed her soul back over to this world.

    Until, that day, something changed.

    One-year-old An Yuning, still learning to walk, accidentally bumped into the edge of the rug, scraping her knee. The pain instantly brought tears to the little one's eyes, and they spilled over at once. She sat down hard on the floor, her small body swaying twice, and almost by instinct stretched both arms toward the figure on the sofa, her cry childish and urgent:

    "Mama... Mama..."

    That voice was both urgent and soft, carrying the purest, most direct dependence.

    An Yilin, who had merely been staring blankly at the pages of her book, felt as though some invisible force had struck her hard the instant she heard that word, "Mama." Those eyes of hers, always so hollow, trembled violently in that moment, her pupils contracting sharply.

    She was not a bystander.

    She was the mother of these two children.

    This was no longer a matter of "being unable to move past grief," nor an excuse of "not wanting to speak." Her child was crying, was bleeding, was calling for her. What the child needed was not a shadow that stayed forever silent, forever hiding in the dark, but someone who would hold her, comfort her gently, and rush to her side the instant she was hurt — a real mother.

    The ice that had built up over that entire year in her chest let out a faint, cracking sound in that moment.

    An Yilin was on her feet almost instantly, striding forward. She knelt on the carpet and swept the sobbing, hiccuping An Yuning into her arms. The child's small hands were still trembling, her tears soaking through the fabric on her mother's shoulder, and that crying drilled straight into her eardrums, jolting awake a heart that had been asleep for far too long.

    She held her daughter, her arms slowly tightening. Looking down at this small life bound to her by blood, An Yilin's eyes finally, bit by bit, began to redden.

    She suddenly understood — she could not go on like this any longer.

    She could not let her children, at an age when they needed a mother, receive only a body with no warmth in it. She was their mother; she had to learn to stand up, learn to speak, learn to love, learn to become a harbor her children could rest their trust in.

    "Don't be scared..."

    The words fell from her lips, her voice terribly hoarse, yet unusually clear.

    It was not mere reflex — it was the first response she had given to this world after a full year of silence. She finally understood that she wasn't destined to live only within her loss; she still had these two children.

    From that day on, An Yilin truly began to "wake up," at least a little.

    She began to draw closer to the children on her own. In the past, it had always been the nanny who carried the children over to her before she would take them out of habit; now, whenever she saw the twins crawling too far across the carpet, she would instinctively reach out and pull them back to her side.

    An Yuchen was shy around strangers, yet clung to her with unusual attachment. Whenever he stumbled and fell, holding back his breath, refusing to cry, only shuffling over to press against her side and murmur a muffled "Mama," An Yilin would gently bend down, carefully checking his scrape, a flicker of a mother's focused attention finally surfacing in her eyes.

    An Yuning cried easily but laughed just as easily. The moment she managed to grab hold of the hem of her mother's clothes, her tears would stop instantly, as though the world were safe as long as she was in her mother's arms.

    These small, fragmentary changes did not go unnoticed by the An family.

    Shen Tinglan would often watch from a distance by the door, her smile carrying a bittersweet relief; An Jingche, passing by the stairwell now and then, would catch sight of the figure in the living room holding a child, and the coldness in his eyes would soften, if only for an instant; An Jingchuan, coming home after finishing his work, would see that his sister was no longer an empty shell, and the stone that had long weighed on his heart would finally settle.

    Ji Yunshen watched all of this too.

    Over this past year, the frequency of his visits to the An family had not decreased, but the place where he stood was, little by little, moving forward.

    In the past, he would mostly stop at the door or in the hallway, glancing over to make sure she was all right before turning to leave. Now, he would sit on the single-seat sofa a little ways from where the children played, handling his work, occasionally lifting his gaze, which would drift, almost involuntarily, toward her, if only to catch one more glimpse.

    This time, when An Yuning scraped her knee again and cried hard from the pain, An Yilin rushed over almost reflexively, pulling the child onto her lap, pressing a cartoon bandage over the scrape while soothing her in a low voice: "If it hurts, go ahead and cry, it's all right, Mama's here."

    That voice was very gentle, very genuine.

    Standing in the doorway, Ji Yunshen heard that word "Mama," and his chest gave a sudden, violent jolt. In that moment, he felt an overwhelming sense of reality — she really was trying, trying with everything she had to become a mother.

    Night fell, and the children had gone to sleep.

    Only a single floor lamp remained lit in the living room. An Yilin sat on the sofa, holding a design sketch she hadn't touched in a long time, her fingertips slowly gliding across the paper.

    Ji Yunshen walked over and quietly set a plate of peeled apple slices down on the table.

    "Be careful not to forget to eat again," he said, his tone even, as though remarking on the most ordinary thing in the world.

    An Yilin looked up at him. This time, her gaze was no longer so hollow, no longer so distant. She fell silent for a few seconds, then picked up a slice of apple and quietly took a bite.

    It was the first time in this entire year that she had accepted his care in front of him the way a truly "living person" would.

    Ji Yunshen understood perfectly well in his heart.

    She wasn't ready yet to face love. She had only just found the courage to be a mother again. But for him, that was already enough.

    She was moving forward, and all he needed to do was follow her pace — never pushing, never forcing.

    As long as she kept moving forward, he was willing to stay by her side for as long as it took, waiting together for dawn.
