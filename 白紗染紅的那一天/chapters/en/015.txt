Chapter 15: Who Is Pulling the Strings

    The frantic knocking outside the door hadn't stopped, and the atmosphere inside had already stretched to its breaking point.

    An Yilin stood at the top of the stairs holding the children, her face still somewhat pale. Ji Yunshen stood in front of her, his expression cold as though sheathed in frost. The three An brothers stood guard on either side, not one of them willing to give an inch. Just then, a clear, composed woman's voice suddenly cut through from beyond the door — not loud, but carrying enough weight to silence everyone, inside and out.

    "Knocking without an answer, and continuing to harass the occupants regardless, already constitutes disturbing another person's peace. Under Article 72 of the Social Order Maintenance Act, anyone who disturbs another person's residential peace without cause may be penalized accordingly."

    The voice was steady and calm, yet every word came through with perfect clarity, as though reciting statutes in a courtroom, without a trace of excess emotion.

    After a pause of two seconds, the voice continued: "Furthermore, regarding custody and rights concerning children born out of wedlock — the law does not allow grandparents to simply take a child away because they wish to. The primary care and custody arrangements for a child are, by law, to be determined by the parents or by a court. Collateral relatives have no right to forcibly remove a child in the name of blood relation."

    Outside the door, things quieted considerably, and Lin Shuhua's expression shifted along with it.

    The voice paused for a moment before adding, flatly, one last line: "If you don't leave right now, I will call the police directly. At that point, it won't be a matter of asking you to leave — the police will handle it. I'll say it once more: leave immediately."

    The moment those words fell, the people outside the door did indeed pull back somewhat, though they still stood frozen in place, unwilling to accept defeat. The three An brothers exchanged a glance, and An Jingchuan stepped forward first, pulling the door open. Before the people outside could even open their mouths again, they were met with several equally cold gazes from within, and their entire presence instantly crumpled under the weight of it.

    At that moment, Ji Yunshen came down from the top of the stairs, carrying a soundly sleeping An Yuning in his arms. The little girl lay quietly against his shoulder, her breathing even, deep in sleep. He glanced down at the child in his arms, his expression softening in a way rarely seen, and only then handed her back to An Yilin, his voice low. "Take her back to her room first. I'll handle this."

    An Yilin pressed her lips together, glancing at him, then at the unfriendly figures beyond the door, and finally nodded, carefully securing her hold on her daughter before turning to head upstairs. As she climbed the stairs step by step, her retreating figure still carried a note of tension, though calmer than before. Only once her footsteps had faded around the turn of the staircase did Ji Yunshen slowly withdraw his gaze and turn to walk back down.

    Outside the door, Li Yu'en's parents still stood there, their faces growing uglier by the moment. Ji Yunshen approached step by step, his eyes cold as though covered in frost, finally coming to a stop in front of Li Zhengde and seizing his wrist directly — the pressure neither too light nor too heavy, but more than enough to make escape impossible.

    "Talk." His voice was low, carrying almost no fluctuation, yet it pressed down hard enough to tighten the chest. "Who told you?"

    Li Zhengde's face stiffened under that stare, as though he hadn't expected such directness. But before he could speak, An Jingche had already positioned himself on the other side, his gaze sweeping over coldly, and An Jingheng stepped forward as well, blocking off any retreat.

    An Jingchuan stood by the door with his arms crossed, not a trace of a smile on his face, only naked impatience and warning.

    "If you don't name the person today," An Jingchuan said coldly, "you can forget about ever setting foot through this door again."

    Li Zhengde's face flushed and paled by turns under the pressure, and at last, under that gaze cold enough to feel crushing, he gritted his teeth and spoke.

    "It was... it was a woman."

    That single sentence made the expressions of everyone at the door darken all at once.

    Li Zhengde's throat worked, as though the words shamed him even to say, but under that overwhelming pressure, he forced himself to continue: "She said An Yilin secretly had our Li family's children, and said this couldn't just be let go, told us to come take the children back. She also said we shouldn't let An Yilin have it easy — that we should throw her life into chaos, make sure she couldn't live peacefully. She said if we took the children back by force, it would blow up into a huge scandal, and the An family would fall apart on its own."

    When the words fell, dead silence spread outside the door.

    An Jingche's face went cold in an instant, and a visible chill flickered through An Jingheng's eyes as well. An Jingchuan let out a cold laugh outright, as though he'd grown so furious it had turned to amusement instead.

    "So," he said, each word deliberate, "you actually had the nerve to come here and try to snatch the children?"

    Li Zhengde's face twisted, his lips moving, but no words came out for a moment.

    Ji Yunshen didn't loosen his grip — if anything, he leaned in closer, his tone cold as ice.

    "Who is that woman?"

    Li Zhengde's throat seemed to catch on something. He hesitated for several seconds before finally forcing the words out through gritted teeth.

    "She said her surname was Gu. Gu Wanjun."

    The moment that name left his mouth, the air itself seemed to drop several more degrees.

    An Jingche's gaze darkened slightly, and An Jingheng's brow furrowed along with it. An Jingchuan turned his eyes directly on Li Zhengde, his tone dropping to something frighteningly heavy.

    "Say that again. What's her name?"

    Under the weight of those staring eyes, Li Zhengde could only push through, unnerved: "She was dressed very respectably, spoke with a lot of confidence, like she was very familiar with your side of things. She kept smiling the whole time she talked, and said... as long as we made a scene, went and took the children, An Yilin would never be able to live in peace again."

    When those three words — An Yilin — left his mouth, the expressions of everyone from the An family darkened completely.

    This was no longer simply a matter of Li Yu'en's parents losing control and showing up at the door.

    Someone, behind the scenes, had deliberately placed a blade in the Li family's hands, then pushed them forward to draw blood.

    Ji Yunshen's expression turned cold as frost, his fingers slowly tightening, before he finally spoke, one word at a time.

    "Gu Wanjun."

    The voice was very quiet, yet it seemed to drive those three syllables straight into everyone's ears.

    He lifted his eyes, and there was no warmth left in them at all.

    "Very good."
