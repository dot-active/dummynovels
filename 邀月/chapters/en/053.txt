(2) A Parting at the End of Life

The cave, as it turned out, opened into an entirely different world within—without a guide, not a single step forward would have been possible, and it was little wonder He Yan had spoken with such absolute certainty that they could never find their way without her.

Chu Mingxi calculated everything carefully as she walked, quietly instructing Tian Xu to memorize the path as well. She knew that Tian Xu, like herself, possessed a gift for remembering anything he saw at a single glance—if anything were to happen to her within, Tian Xu would still be able to lead Yue Heng and Xiaoba safely back out of the cave.

As she walked, she signaled Tian Xu with a series of covert gestures—a silent language she and her son had cultivated together over more than a decade, understood only between the two of them, so private that not even Yue Heng could decipher it, let alone He Yan. He Yan felt certain that Chu Mingxi's life now rested entirely in her own hands, that even Yue Heng had to tread carefully, watching her every expression before he dared act—how could she possibly imagine that a woman already doomed to defeat could still conjure up any tricks at all?

But she had, in the end, gravely underestimated just what Chu Mingxi was truly capable of. This was a woman who had, without a sound, without so much as a ripple, transformed Tianyin Pavilion into a magnificent, thriving house of commerce, the foremost of its kind throughout the entire Tang empire, benefiting the realm far and wide—a woman Cheng Siyuan himself had once said could rival even Empress Wu, a true wonder of her age. How could such a woman possibly be expected to simply fold her hands and await slaughter? From the time Tian Xu had been just a small child, she had brought him along on the caravan's travels, training him at every turn, preparing him always to one day take up her mantle. She had taught him that even in the most desperate of circumstances, one must remain calm and composed, unshaken in the face of danger—only then might one find a way to survive even the direst of situations. Once, when their caravan had been ambushed by an unknown band of attackers en route, both she and Yue Heng had been gravely wounded, and Tian Xu, only ten years old at the time, had risen to the occasion in that moment of crisis, leading the caravan onward himself, without mother or adoptive father at his side, to meet with the Sogdian traders and complete the exchange exactly as planned. Tian Xu's commanding presence, the bearing of a natural leader, had revealed itself even in his earliest years. No tiger ever bore a weakling for a cub—it was only a shame that He Yan knew nothing of any of this; the gulf in vision and character between He Yan and Chu Mingxi could not have been more plain. A truly skilled player of the game always thinks many moves ahead, anticipating every step the opponent might take, and dangles bait that the enemy cannot resist, drawing them into overconfidence until they let their guard down entirely without even realizing it—and this was exactly what Chu Mingxi had done. She knew that to have any chance of truly seeing Cheng Siyuan again, she would have to pay the price with her own life; beyond that sacrifice, He Yan stood to gain nothing more from her at all, with only utter ruin waiting ahead—and so she had swallowed the poison without the slightest hesitation.

After roughly half an hour of walking, a residence at the far end of the cave suddenly came into view before them, bathed in brilliant sunlight. There were guards posted both inside and around the compound, though not many of them—for one thing, Cheng Siyuan, ensnared by the love-gu, had no hope of escape even if he tried, since anyone poisoned by it could not survive if separated too far from the mother-gu; for another, Cheng Siyuan's health had been deteriorating steadily, no longer the vigorous, formidably skilled martial artist he had once been, and so there was little need to keep him under heavy guard at all.

He Yan, familiar with every turn of the path, led them straight to a small courtyard named "Sleeping Dragon." The whole compound, inside and out, was strangely, profoundly silent, with nothing but the chirping of insects, the calls of birds, and the faint patter of falling leaves breaking the stillness. He Yan gave a slight jerk of her chin, indicating that Cheng Siyuan lay within, and Chu Mingxi rushed forward at once toward the building, with Yue Heng, Tian Xu, and Xiaoba hurrying close behind to protect her.

"A-Yuan..." Chu Mingxi called out as she ran, all the way into the inner chamber, where she saw a man lying beneath a thick quilt upon the bed, his face deathly pale and utterly drained of color, his eyes tightly shut. Hearing her voice, he snapped his eyes open at once and struggled with all his remaining strength to sit up, only to collapse back onto the bed, his body too weak to hold him.

"Xi'er? Is this... an illusion?" Cheng Siyuan murmured weakly to himself.

"A-Yuan... it's Xi'er... Xi'er has come too late..."

Chu Mingxi dissolved into tears. Though she had braced herself for this moment, seeing Cheng Siyuan's gaunt, ravaged face before her still struck her heart like a blade, and grief overwhelmed her completely. She rushed to the edge of the bed and sat down, straining to lift Cheng Siyuan up, only to discover that the man who had once been so full of vigor, so broad-shouldered and sturdy that not even her utmost strength could ever budge him an inch, had now wasted away to skin and bone, his breath so faint it seemed to hang by the thinnest of threads, each breath a visible struggle—had it not been for the last remaining traces of spirited brightness still lingering in his brow and eyes, she might scarcely have recognized him at all. She drew Cheng Siyuan's body close against her own, gently stroking his face.

"You've suffered so much. Xi'er and our son have come to take you home." Chu Mingxi sniffed back her tears and beckoned to Tian Xu. "Xu'er, come here! Greet your father."

"Xu'er?" A note of astonished delight crept into Cheng Siyuan's frail voice, as though sudden color had flooded back into him all at once. He gazed at Tian Xu, drawing closer step by step, his eyes brimming with light.

"Your son pays his respects to Father..." Tian Xu walked to the bedside and knelt, bowing with proper formality, his forehead striking the floor three times in solemn respect. Though utterly drained of strength, Cheng Siyuan still strained to reach out his hand toward him.

"Good..."

Cheng Siyuan's hand hovered, trembling, in midair, and seeing this, Tian Xu crawled forward on his knees and took hold of it, murmuring softly, "Father." Tears welled instantly in Cheng Siyuan's eyes. Yue Heng and Xiaoba, standing to one side, felt their hearts break with sorrow; Xiaoba could no longer hold back and began to weep, rushing forward to kneel beside Tian Xu, bowing his head low. "That the young master has suffered so terribly is Xiaoba's own failing, for not having remained at the young master's side..."

"No... you protected... my wife... and my son... thank you... and A-Heng too... thank you..."

Before Cheng Siyuan could even finish speaking, his face suddenly changed color, and he clutched at his chest, coughing up mouthful after mouthful of blood. Startled, Yue Heng rushed several steps closer, only to hear He Yan point at Chu Mingxi and laugh. "You are my eldest martial brother's death warrant. All these years, the mere thought of you has been enough to tear him apart with pain—now that he's actually looking at you, wouldn't that be just the thing to finish him off?"

Hearing this, Yue Heng lashed out furiously with a kick. He Yan crumpled to the ground in pain, and Cheng Siyuan, overcome, lost consciousness entirely. Chu Mingxi clutched him tightly to her, while Xiaoba and Tian Xu scrambled up to shield him as well. Yue Heng leapt lightly onto the bed and began channeling his own internal energy into Cheng Siyuan's body, and after a long, agonizing while, Cheng Siyuan's eyes slowly fluttered open once more.

"Heaven... has shown... mercy... letting me... see... Xi'er... once... more..." Cheng Siyuan's voice came weak, halting, and broken, each word wrenched out with tremendous effort. Chu Mingxi understood at once what he meant—the reason he had never sought death was that he had clung to the hope that one day he might see her again, and it was that very hope alone that had carried him through such unbearable agony, all the way to this moment. Now that his wish had been fulfilled at last, he no longer feared death, and his heart was filled instead with gratitude for heaven's mercy. Her A-Yuan—he had always been, and would forever remain, a man of such gentle, boundless kindness! Even having witnessed the darkest, most corrupt secrets of the imperial household and court, even after enduring more than a decade of torment as harrowing as any hell, he had lived his whole life with clarity and light undimmed, his entire existence like that of a compassionate bodhisattva, using the light within himself to illuminate others, and in the end, illuminating himself as well. Chu Mingxi felt the urge to weep, but instead she offered Cheng Siyuan a smile and said, "Just as you wished—everyone is well."

Cheng Siyuan seemed to understand, and nodded, a look of comfort settling over his face. He Yan, watching the two of them so perfectly attuned to one another's hearts, felt a bitter twist within her, and spoke up in a tone of malicious anticipation. "She swallowed poison just to come see my eldest martial brother—she won't be living much longer either..." No sooner had she said this than everyone watched, taken utterly by surprise, as Chu Mingxi told Tian Xu and Xiaoba to steady Cheng Siyuan, and then, in that one unguarded instant, triggered a hidden mechanism—a dart shot out from her left sleeve, piercing straight through He Yan's left breast, drawing a chorus of startled gasps from everyone present.

"This time, I'm not leaving..." Amid everyone's cries of shock, and beneath He Yan's uncomprehending stare as she collapsed to the ground, Chu Mingxi remained composed and calm, still gently stroking Cheng Siyuan's face with a smile.

"It's... you... Xi'er..." Cheng Siyuan gazed at Chu Mingxi, his eyes filled with tenderness, with aching love, but more than anything, with understanding. The two of them had once vowed to stand together through life and death alike—but the last time, unwilling to let A-Heng and Xi'er, along with the child still growing within her, be dragged needlessly into danger, he had chosen, in that moment of crisis, to face his fate alone, breaking his promise and sending Yue Heng to take her away instead. This time, at last, Xi'er had been able to make her own choice, to share in life and death together, on her own terms.

This was who she was—Chu Mingxi, a woman who had lived her whole life with decisive resolve, masterful strategy, capable of determining the outcome of any contest from a thousand miles away.

"Do you still remember, A-Yuan? That day when you rescued the caravan from the mountain bandits, and I later asked whether the Jinxiu Fabric Shop might offer double the going rate to find me guards of exceptional martial skill? I wanted to push past Tibetan-held Longyou territory and continue along the old trade routes to deal with the Sogdians." Taking Cheng Siyuan back from Tian Xu and Xiaoba's hands once more, Chu Mingxi spoke slowly, unhurried.

Cheng Siyuan gave a faint, effortful nod, his gaze settling on the pale bluish-white jade bird-and-flower hairpin in Chu Mingxi's hair—the very one he had fastened into Xi'er's hair with his own hands on their wedding day.

"You were remarkably straightforward about it—you said you, too, made your living dealing in information, that you had connections everywhere, that you'd run your own trade routes through the Longyou region for years without hindrance, and men enough under your command; and I had money, and was willing to pay a generous price for it—it truly was an excellent arrangement for us both. To this day, we're nearly the only merchant house left in the whole of the Tang empire still able to keep that trade route running—and that's entirely thanks to you."

Chu Mingxi spoke on calmly, and seeing Cheng Siyuan's eyelids lower, a faint stir passing over him, she continued, "After Father passed away, my stepmother seized every last coin and the entire Jinxiu Fabric Shop for herself—all that remained untouched was the house Mother had left me before she died, which my stepmother had no claim over at all. I decided then to sell that house to buy carriages, and to hire guards so I could go negotiate a continued partnership with the Shu brocade family we'd worked with before. My stepmother and half-brother had always lived a life of ease and comfort, never once traveling with the caravans themselves, never once negotiating a deal on their own—how could they possibly understand any of it? In less than two years, I'd managed to buy that house back at exactly the price I'd sold it for. I thought myself simply fortunate at the time, but afterward, Xiaoba couldn't help but let slip the truth to me in secret—that it was you who had quietly arranged for someone to purchase it, so that when I later wished to buy it back, I wouldn't have to pay a single coin more than what I'd received for it. You said it was only a small favor, just watching over the house my mother had left me..."

Chu Mingxi gazed at Cheng Siyuan; his breathing had grown shallower and shallower now, though a faint smile still touched his lips. Holding back the grief welling within her, she spoke on, softly. "You asked me whether I was willing to become your wife, the wife of Tianyin Pavilion's own Pavilion Master—knowing full well the court could seize the two of you at any moment, that danger lurked at every turn. I told you I was willing to share your fortune and misfortune alike, to stand with you through life and death together—Xi'er was never a weak woman, but your equal, walking always by your side..."

As she spoke these final words, the rise and fall of Cheng Siyuan's chest went still; his arm slipped limply downward, and nothing remained but the faint trace of that smile lingering upon his lips. At last, the tears in Chu Mingxi's eyes spilled over...

But almost immediately, a bitter chill swept through Chu Mingxi's entire body, and she began to tremble uncontrollably, even as a searing, burning heat surged simultaneously within her belly. That agonizing collision of extreme cold and extreme heat churned relentlessly through every part of her body.

"Mother..." Tian Xu, standing closest to Chu Mingxi, sensed at once that something was terribly wrong, and immediately handed Cheng Siyuan over to Xiaoba beside him, wrapping his arms tightly around Chu Mingxi instead.

"Xi'er..." Yue Heng freed his hands at once, taking Chu Mingxi from Tian Xu's arms, sweeping her up and leaping down from the bed, ready to rush outside.

"Hold on just a little longer—I'm fast on my feet, and old Zhou is right at the foot of the mountain. I'll get you to him right now." Yue Heng was frantic with panic—he had felt this same helpless dread only once before, the day both his parents had died.

"Too... late... it's... too late... A-Heng... stop..."

"No..." Though Yue Heng protested aloud, he had never once, in all his life, defied Chu Mingxi's wishes, and so he came to a halt, sinking to his knees on the ground, holding tightly to her trembling, convulsing body, utterly at a loss for what to do.

With great difficulty, Chu Mingxi raised her hand, drawing Tian Xu's own hand—he, too, now kneeling beside her—forward until it rested in Yue Heng's grasp, and forced out, with tremendous effort, "The child... and the manor... I entrust... to you... Xu'er... must... honor... you as... his own... father..." Before she could even finish speaking, Chu Mingxi coughed up several mouthfuls of blood, a thin trickle seeping from the corner of her eye as well, and her hand, drained of all strength, went limp and slipped away, releasing Tian Xu's hand entirely...

Chapter Nineteen: One Stone, Three Birds

"Eldest Martial Brother... Sister-in-law..." Yu Shurong murmured under his breath, and for a long while, no one present could pull themselves back from the crushing weight of shock and grief. But then a sudden commotion arose from just outside the main gate of the Cao residence, forcing everyone back from that dark, heavy silence. A squad of ten men from the Divine Strategy Army had arrived at the county magistrate's gate, torches blazing, pounding to be let in—and at their head was none other than the Left Flank Protector-General of the Divine Strategy Army and Overseer of Palace Affairs, Dou Wenchang himself. This sudden, entirely improper arrival, without so much as a word of prior notice, nearly frightened Cao Jibin out of his wits, and he could only steel himself, sweat soaking through his robes, and hurry out to receive this most unwelcome and formidable of guests. Dou Wenchang's face was dark with fury, and without wasting a single word of preamble, he said, "My unworthy son has behaved disgracefully, paying a nighttime visit to a young lady of your household. I've come to bring him home."

As it happened, Dou Wenchang had received word from Guanguan that the young master had slipped out of the house dressed in night-travel garb. Had this simply been another night of pleasure-seeking in Pingkang Ward, as was his usual habit, he would never have seemed so suspicious about it—which led her to suspect he had fallen for someone, and that this someone might well be Magistrate Cao's own Fourth Sister Guo. That visit to the secret chamber the other day, she suspected, had been on her account, and it was because of her that the young master had come away injured.

Guanguan spoke with such conviction, and utter certainty. She was a concubine Dou Wenchang had given to Yu Shurong, and though Yu Shurong's dalliances throughout Pingkang Ward had never particularly troubled her—after all, it was nothing more than idle amusement to him, and she hadn't taken it especially to heart. But tonight's furtive behavior struck her as suspicious beyond reason. Though Yu Shurong had never cared for her, she herself had fallen hopelessly, obsessively in love with him, and the moment she suspected he had found someone he truly cared for, a fierce jealousy overtook her, and she rushed to report everything to Dou Wenchang, hoping to see the budding romance crushed before it could bloom. Though the information Guanguan had given him was mistaken, it had, quite by accident, alerted Dou Wenchang to the fact that Yu Shurong was acting behind his back—whether it was truly a love affair, or something even more serious than that, he couldn't be certain, but he knew better than to take the matter lightly.

Tian Xu and the others heard the commotion at the Cao residence quite clearly. At first, they turned suspicious eyes toward Yu Shurong, who shook his head urgently. "It wasn't me... believe me... I have no idea what's happening here..." Then he added, "This is no place to linger. Go, now, quickly—don't let that old villain discover you. Even Fourth Sister Guo must leave at once. Leave the rest to me."

Tian Xu and the others remained only half-convinced, but regardless, they needed to withdraw immediately. The group, with Guo Huilian in tow, leapt up onto the rooftops together and retraced their earlier path back to the Four Seas Inn. Yu Shurong turned and made his way out of the courtyard, and from a distance he could already see Dou Wenchang's face dark with fury, with Cao Jibin standing beside him, bowing deeply.

"Where is she?" Dou Wenchang demanded, struggling to hold back his anger.

"It wouldn't be appropriate for her to come forward." Yu Shurong understood at once that Dou Wenchang meant Guo Huilian, and answered coldly. Then, turning to face Magistrate Cao directly, he said in a low, steady voice, "I was the one who abducted those people, purely for my own amusement. As for seizing the magistrate's own wife, that too was meant to teach the magistrate a lesson for his eagerness to make a name for himself the moment he took office, showing neither my adoptive father nor myself the proper respect. I admit it all... and that so-called foreign merchant was nothing more than a decoy, meant to throw everyone off the scent." With that, he drew a pill from his sleeve and swallowed it whole. Dou Wenchang broke out in a cold sweat—he himself had been the true mastermind behind all of this, hoping both to put Cao Jibin in his place, to remind him that here in the capital, it was Dou Wenchang who held the final say, and, at the same time, hoping the scheme might flush out members of Tianyin Pavilion in the process. And now, here was Yu Shurong, stepping forward to shoulder the blame himself, allowing Cao Jibin to close the case cleanly and report success—leaving Dou Wenchang utterly unable to voice the bitter injustice of it all. He knew perfectly well that Yu Shurong was concealing something, and yet, having just swallowed the very poison every death-sworn agent under his command carried at all times, this puppet he had so carefully, painstakingly cultivated from childhood, all in hopes of one day seizing control of Tianyin Pavilion through him, had now turned the tables on him instead—silencing himself forever beyond any hope of interrogation, and, in that single moment of swallowing the poison, utterly extinguishing decades of hope that Tianyin Pavilion might ever be reclaimed...

Chapter Twenty: The Song Ends, But the Crowd Remains

Good news arrived at the Qingle Tower in Pingkang Ward: the medicinal herbs sought all the way from the Longyou region had finally taken effect, and under Zhou Mengda's careful treatment, Yu Shurong, who had lain unconscious for three full months, finally awoke. But he had lost all memory of who he was—he couldn't even recall his own name—and so Zhou Mengda simply began calling him Xu Ye once more.

Losing every memory entirely might, perhaps, have been something of a blessing after all—no longer would he have to recall those days of deliberately playing the arrogant, unruly troublemaker, forever giving Dou Wenchang headaches and forcing him to clean up after him. Yue Heng had harbored doubts about Yu Shurong at the time, and so had doubled back to hide nearby, watching that entire scene unfold in secret. Once Dou Wenchang had departed, he'd immediately given Yu Shurong the antidote pill that everyone had carried on their person ever since Chu Mingxi's death by poison, the one Zhou Mengda had distributed to them all. Though it couldn't neutralize every possible poison, it was enough to suppress the toxin's effects and buy him precious time to find a proper cure. Yue Heng had then carried Yu Shurong on his back, racing through the night to find Zhou Mengda, and it was this alone that had saved his life.

With Yueyue Manor's assistance, Cao Jibin came to understand the full truth of the affair. Because the matter involved the son of a court official, and though Yu Shurong had taken the blame upon himself, the fact that Dou Wenchang was secretly the true proprietor behind Yun Xiang Garden was brought fully into the light as a result. Amid the outrage of his assembled ministers, Emperor Dezong himself lashed out furiously at Dou Wenchang, reprimanding him harshly for failing to properly discipline his own son, and docking his salary for half a year as punishment. Because of this, Dou Wenchang didn't dare go and claim the body himself, instead instructing Cao Jibin to handle the remains on his own. And with Yu Shurong having single-handedly wrecked decades of careful planning and scheming, how could Dou Wenchang possibly still regard him as his own adopted son? His heart burned with bitter resentment, and from beginning to end, he never once came forward to show any concern for Yu Shurong's remains at all.

As for the concubine Guanguan, being a woman of neither formal status nor standing, whether she would be permitted to claim Yu Shurong's body was, naturally, entirely up to Cao Jibin to decide. And so Cao Jibin and Tian Xu were able to join forces seamlessly, hiding him instead within the Qingle Tower in Pingkang Ward—the very headquarters where intelligence from across the realm converged.

By the time the carriage arrived at Yueyue Manor, a crowd had already gathered thick outside the gates—old women, young ladies, servant boys, all of them eager for a glimpse of "the most beautiful man in all Chang'an." Han Jin wore an expression of open disapproval, while Yueming, gripping Han Qian with one hand and Yuelan with the other, pushed her way to the very front of the crowd, and even she stood there, eyes bright with eager anticipation, at the sight of Guo Huilian standing beside Tian Xu—which left Tian Xu's own eyes brimming with unmistakable jealousy. The moment that pale blue-green round-collared robe came into view, gasps of admiration rippled through the crowd one after another, an unbroken wave of astonishment at his beauty that showed no sign of ever dying down...

Though Xu Ye retained no memory whatsoever, he seemed strangely at ease amid the crowd's staring, showing no trace of awkwardness at all. Accompanied by Zhou Mengda, he strode forward, and from a distance he caught sight of Tian Xu standing at the very front of the gathered crowd.

"Welcome home."

Tian Xu greeted him with a warm, welcoming smile.

Xu Ye gazed at his face, and came to a sudden stop, frozen for a long moment in stunned silence.

"Eldest Martial Brother," he said at last, his smile breaking out as bright and radiant as the sun itself.
